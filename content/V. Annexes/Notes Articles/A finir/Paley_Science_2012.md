## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article defined two populations of exhausted T cells based on the mutual expression of either Tbet or Eomes. The Eomes populations seemed more exhausted and could not differenciate into Tbet, but the opposite is possible. KO of each TF resulted in the accumulation of the other population. Both population are important to control disease and the depletion of one reduce protection. Finally, Tbet population decrease with time, suggesting a finite pool that slowly proliferate and give rise to Eomes population. [[1.3 Dysfonction]]
> 
>**Experiment** : Cl13 chronic infection
>**Results** : Upregulation of Eomes with exhaustion, and downregulation of Tbet. Upregulation of Blimp1 and ICB. Eomes+ had less cytokine secretion. Eomes+ proliferate more than Tbet+
>
>**Experiment** : KO of Eomes or Tbet
>**Results** : Tbet KO resulted in higher Eomes/PD1 and lower proliferation. Eomes KO resulted in lower PD1 and Blimp1 and proliferation and higher Tbet and cytokines.
>
>**Experiment** : Use of PD1 as surrogate marker; sorting of PD1hi and low and transfer
>**Results** : PD1low converted into PD1hi and had high proliferation, but not the opposite.
>
>**Experiment** : KO of Eomes or Tbet
>**Results** : The loss of either lead to reduced protection.
>
>**Other Results** : There is a loss of Tbet population over time, suggesting that it slowly differenciate into Eomes population.
>
>**Other Results** : The populations are also found in HCV infected human individuals.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Chronic infections strain the regenerative capacity of antiviral T lymphocyte populations, leading to failure in long-term immunity. The cellular and molecular events controlling this regenerative capacity, however, are unknown. We found that two distinct states of virus-specific CD8(+) T cells exist in chronically infected mice and humans. Differential expression of the T-box transcription factors T-bet and Eomesodermin (Eomes) facilitated the cooperative maintenance of the pool of antiviral CD8(+) T cells during chronic viral infection. T-bet(hi) cells displayed low intrinsic turnover but proliferated in response to persisting antigen, giving rise to Eomes(hi) terminal progeny. Genetic elimination of either subset resulted in failure to control chronic infection, which suggests that an imbalance in differentiation and renewal could underlie the collapse of immunity in humans with chronic infections.
