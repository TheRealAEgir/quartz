> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : MDA5 = RLR that detect viral DNA/RNA. Talk about effect of type I IFN on CD8 T cells exhaustion and chronic LCMV infection [[1.3 Dysfonction]]
> 
>**Experiment** : BDAC2-DTR mouse in which pDC can be depleted by DT and infection with LCMV Cl13 and Arm.
>**Results** : IFNa response following infection that was impacted only at early timpoint by pDC depletion. No effect of depletion on virus titers nor CD8 T cells.
>
>**Experiment** : Study of pathways; WT vs TRIF KO vs MyD88 KO vs MDA5 KO mouse
>**Results** : High decrease in MDA5 KO of IFNa secretion, suggesting that pDC sense virus through MDA5 instead of TLR7/9. Early production depended on TRL7/MyD88. MDA5 KO mouse lacked type I IFN responses.
>
>**Other Results** : MDA5 KO mouse had less T cell expansion, reduction in gp33 specific T cells
>
>**Other Results** : No differences in IL12 production in MDA5 KO, no effect of IL12 blockade.
>
>**Experiment** : + CD4 depletion
>**Results** : further reduction of gp33 specific T cells and cytokine production, increase PD1 expression.
>
>**Experiment** : Earl IFN I and CD4 depletion
>**Results** : -> exhaustion in CD8 T cells.
>
>**Other Results** : Rapid decline of IFNa production in Cl13 infection
>
>**Experiment** : Exogenous administration of type I IFN
>**Results** : more gp33-specific T cells in WT mouse, restored MDA5 KO to a level similar to WT. Smeed important during priming phase. CD4 depletion did not diminished efficacy. Better protection.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Type I Interferons (IFN-I) promote antiviral CD8+T cell responses, but the contribution of different IFN-I sources and signaling pathways are ill-defined. While plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) produce IFN-I upon TLR stimulation, IFN-I are induced in most cells by helicases like MDA5. Using acute and chronic lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV) infection models, we determined that pDCs transiently produce IFN-I that minimally impacts CD8+T cell responses and viral persistence. Rather, MDA5 is the key sensor that induces IFN-I required for CD8+T cell responses. In the absence of MDA5, CD8+T cell responses to acute infection rely on CD4+T cell help, and loss of both CD4+T cells and MDA5 results in CD8+T cell exhaustion and persistent infection. Chronic LCMV infection rapidly attenuates IFN-I responses, but early administration of exogenous IFN-I rescues CD8+T cells, promoting viral clearance. Thus, effective antiviral CD8+T cell responses depend on the timing and magnitude of IFN-I responses.
