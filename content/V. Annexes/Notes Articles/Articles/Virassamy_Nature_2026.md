## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article observed that there is an accumulation of CD8+ T cells with a TRM phenotype following breastfeeding in healthy breast tissue. This was then modelised on mouse, where it was shown that there is a slower CD8-dependant tumor grotwh in breastfeeding group vs no-breastfeeding and parous vs nulliprous. Tcirc were still important because FTY720 resulted in partial acceleration. Finally, in human TNBC, there is higher infiltration of T cells with a similar phenotype when breastfeeded, which correlated with better survival. [[2.5 TRM Cancer]]
> 
>**Experiment** : Healthy breast tissue infiltration
>**Results** : Higher infiltration of T cells and TRM-like when parous in single-cell and FC and microscopy. There is a correlation between age, years since last birth and TRM number. Transcriptomic showed upregulation of TRM genes.
>
>**Experiment** : Mouse model
>**Results** : There is more T cells with a TRM phenotype infiltration when the mouse had done natural lactation.
>**Other Results** : Tumor growth is slower in mouse with natural lactation. Tumor contained more antigen-specific TRM-like cells.
>**Other Results** : Differences in tumor growth were lost when there was no NK/T/B cells, and could be restored by transfer of CD8 T cells. This was also true when WT mouse was depleted of CD8 T cells. FTY720 resulted in lower protection, suggesting that Tcirc are important for this mecanism.
>
>**Experiment** : Back in human TNBC
>**Results** : Higher infiltration of immune cells in parous vs nulliparous. Cells had similar transcriptome than in healthy tissue. Patient who breastfeeded had better survival. There is correlation between TIL infiltrate and healthy breast tisssue TRM signature in TCGA and with OS in METABRIC.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Parity and breastfeeding reduce the risk of breast cancer, particularly triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC),, yet the immunological mechanisms underlying this protection remain unclear. Here we show that parity is associated with increased numbers of CD8+ T cells, including cells with a tissue-resident-memory-like phenotype within human normal breast tissue. In mouse models, pregnancy followed by lactation and involution drove the accumulation of CD8+ T cells in the mammary gland, coinciding with reduced tumour growth and increased intratumoural immune cell infiltration, effects that were abrogated by CD8+ T cell depletion. Importantly, this CD8+ T-cell-dependent tumour control was observed only after a complete cycle of lactation and involution. Consistent with this, primary triple-negative breast cancers from parous women exhibited greater T cell infiltration and improved clinical outcomes. Together, these findings, spanning preclinical models and over 1,000 patient samples, provide insights into how reproductive history shapes breast immunity, positioning CD8+ T cells as key mediators of parity-associated protection and informing strategies for both the prevention and the treatment of breast cancer., Parity induces an accumulation of CD8+ T cells, including cells with a tissue-resident-memory-like phenotype within human normal breast tissue, offering long-term protection against triple-negative breast cancer.
