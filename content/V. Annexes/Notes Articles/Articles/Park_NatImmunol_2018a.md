## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article studied TRM response to infection. They generated TRM using *in vitro* activation, then transfer into mouse and recruitment into skin *via* DNFB. THey showed : 1. Local protection against virus with minimal Tcirc recruitment. 2. Morphology change from dendritic-like to round and motility decrease after activation, and cluster arount infected cells. 3. Minimal recirculation. 4. Acquisition of a inhibitory phenotype after activation, with proliferation and apoptosis resulting in stable number of TRM. 5. Bystander formation of TRM if activated Teff are transfered, which does not decrease the TRM number in skin. [[2.2 Fonctions TRM]] [[2.4 Caractérisation TRM]]
> 
>**Experiment** : *In vitro* activation of gBT-I with gB peptide to "generate" Teff, then injection into mouse and recrutement into the skin *via* DNFB injection. The ganglia is unpopulated by TRM. Then infection by HSV.
>**Results** : Local protection mediated by TRM in the DNFB-treated TRM containing regions.
>**Other Results** : Study of recruitment of CD103- T cells showed that DNFB untreated had higher recruitement.
>**Experiment** : Transfer of increasing doses of Thy1.1 gBT-I to generate TRM and Thy1.1 depletion treatment 
>**Results** : Increasing doses of Thy1.1 gT-I made increasing amount of TRM, and increased protection.
>
>**Experiment** : GFP+ gBT-I and same experiment, then *in vivo* microscopy
>**Results** : Dendritic morphology, slow migration, only in epidermis. After antigen activation, but not with irrelevant antigen, TRM changed to rounded morphology and reduced motility. When infection with HSV-mCherry, gBT-I but not OVA TRM clustered around infected mCherry+ cells and showed reduced motility. This was TRM specific and TCIRC depletion beforehand did not change the results, except for the loss of a dermis super motile T cell population
>
>**Experiment** : Kaede gBT-I cells transfer into mouse (activated + DNFB), infection with HSV-CFP, then photoconversion with UV light.
>**Results** : Accumulation of Kaede-red and Kaede-green gBT-I arount CFP+ loci. Lower velocity of kaede-red. Most skin gBT-I were kaede-red by 3 dpi, but not after 5 dpi. Only kaede-red were CD69+CD103+, and they were never found in lymph node.
>
>**Experiment** : FC of previous experiment
>**Results** : Higher PD1, TIM3, LAG3, CD101, CD244, CTLA4 and ICOS in TRM compared to TCIRCM.
>**Experiment** : Tcirc depletion before infection
>**Results** : No recruitment of CD103- in skin compared to control
>**Experiment** : BrdU incorporation or Ki67 access, then infection to access TRM proliferative capacities.
>**Results** : TRM have some proliferation, and remained CD103+. However, they showed high apoptosis, and so TRM number did not increase.
>
>**Experiment** : To test the impact of Tcircm on Trm mediated protection; isolation of gBT-I Tcircm for the spleen of a mouse, then transfer in a mouse with skin TRM gBT-I.
>**Results** : De novo formation of TRM from Tcircm *via* CD69/CD103 upregulation. Previous TRM number did not change.
>
>**Experiment** : Mouse with gBT-I TRM, then activated OT-I Teff transfer, then HSV infection in the skin
>**Results** : There is recruitment of bystander OT-I T cells in the skin, and they convert into a CD69+CD103+ TRM phenotype.
>
>**Experiment** : To test if there is a "maximum" capacity of TRM number in the skin, injection of a increasing number of Teff and DNFB injection. Then, activation and generation of other antigen specific T cells in other tissues
>**Results** : TRM number in skin plateaued at 100x10^6 transfered cells. Minimal loss of "old" TRM after "new" TRM generation.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Although tissue-resident memory T cells (TRM cells) are critical in fighting infection, their fate after local pathogen re-encounter is unknown. Here we found that skin TRM cells engaged virus-infected cells, proliferated in situ in response to local antigen encounter and did not migrate out of the epidermis, where they exclusively reside. As a consequence, secondary TRM cells formed from pre-existing TRM cells, as well as from precursors recruited from the circulation. Newly recruited antigen-specific or bystander TRM cells were generated in the skin without displacement of the pre-existing TRM cell pool. Thus, pre-existing skin TRM cell populations are not displaced after subsequent infections, which enables multiple TRM cell specificities to be stably maintained within the tissue.
> >.
