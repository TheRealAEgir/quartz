> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article describe that CXCR6 expression for the localisation of TRM in the airways. [[2. Hypothèses TIL]] [[2.4 Caractérisation TRM]] 
> 
>**Experiment** : Study of lung TRM after IAV infection
>**Results** : Similar proportion of NP/PA specific T cells in lung and airways, but more CD11a and Ly6C and lower cytokine secretion in airways TRM. More CXCR6 in lung and in TRM compared to CD69-CD103-. Also true in human.
>
>**Experiment** : CXCR6 expression kinetic
>**Results** : Maximum in early acute response, but maintain through memory phases. Higher in CD69+.
>**Experiment** : Etablishment of circulating memory, then pull in lung by either CpG or antigen + CpG
>**Results** : Higher CXCR6 expression when pull used antigen, suggesting that antigen recognition can impact CXCR6 expression.
>
>**Experiment** : Infection of WT or CXCR6 KO mouse with IAV and study of BAL/lung/spleen at acute and memory phase.
>**Results** : Less NP-specific and total memory T cells in the BAL in both acute and memory in CXCR6 KO mouse. Same result with another antigen. There was no differences in CXCR3 expression. There was reduced protection in CXCR6 KO mouse after re-challenge. These results were replicated with BM mixing and Sendai virus and IAV-OVA (+OT-I) infections, were there is less memory T cells in the airways (and in the liver).
>
>**Experiment** : Study of CXCL16 expression
>**Results** : Only on epithelial cells were it is constitutive. Some expression among alveolar macrophages. Also in human. CXCL16 KO mouse phenocopied CXCR6 KO mouse.
>
>**Experiment** : Study of mechanism responsible for less cells in CXCR6 KO
>**Results** : Survival was not affected (co-transfer directly into lung), but microscopy showed relative distance to airways was impacted, suggesting differences in trafficking.
>
>**Experiment** : Despite CXCR6 KO only affecting airways, CXCR6 expression in lower in airway than in lung
>**Results** : Expression seem to decrease after tissue entry
>
>**Experiment** : Anti-CXCL16 treatment
>**Results** : phenocopied previous results.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Lung TRM cells are present in both the interstitium and airways, but factors regulating their localization to these distinct sites are unknown. This work shows that the CXCR6/CXCL16 axis governs the partitioning of TRM cells to different compartments of the lung and maintains the airway TRM cell pool., Resident memory T cells (TRM cells) are an important first-line defense against respiratory pathogens, but the unique contributions of lung TRM cell populations to protective immunity and the factors that govern their localization to different compartments of the lung are not well understood. Here, we show that airway and interstitial TRM cells have distinct effector functions and that CXCR6 controls the partitioning of TRM cells within the lung by recruiting CD8 TRM cells to the airways. The absence of CXCR6 significantly decreases airway CD8 TRM cells due to altered trafficking of CXCR6−/− cells within the lung, and not decreased survival in the airways. CXCL16, the ligand for CXCR6, is localized primarily at the respiratory epithelium, and mice lacking CXCL16 also had decreased CD8 TRM cells in the airways. Finally, blocking CXCL16 inhibited the steady-state maintenance of airway TRM cells. Thus, the CXCR6/CXCL16 signaling axis controls the localization of TRM cells to different compartments of the lung and maintains airway TRM cells.
