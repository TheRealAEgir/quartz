## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article is the first to identify CXCL16 as a new CXC chemokine, and also identified it's receptor and named it CXCR6. CXCL16 is expressed as mRNA in spleen, LN and peyer's patches, and in NLT (lung, SI kidney, low expression in liver, no expression in brain). The protein expression is restricted to DC (enriched in CD11c+) and is increase after activation of DC (with LSP, anti-CD40, poly(I:C)). CXCL16 has a transmembrane domain, and a intracytoplasmic domain with a potential tyrosine phosphorylase and SH2 protein interaction. It has two form in WB and seems to be heavily glycosylated. It attract activated CD8 T cells and to a lesser degree activated CD4 T cells, but not resting T cells, B cells, macrophages and neutrophils. The attraction trigger a calcium fulx and is sensitive to pertussis toxin. The receptor was identified and named CXCR6, and was previously described as a co-receptor for HIV named Bonzo. [[2. Hypothèses TIL]]
> 
>**Experiment** : Search in public domain databases for sequence with chemokine homology
>**Results** : Discovery of a transmembrane chemokine gene, named CXCL16. There is a intracytoplasmic domain which may contain a tyrosine phosphorylation and a SH2 bind site. Also worked on murine version of this gene.
>
>**Experiment** : Northern blot for tissue location
>**Results** : High expression in spleen, LN, peyer's patches and thymus. In NLT : lung, SI, kidney. No expression in liver/brain
>
>**Experiment** : Flag-CXCL16 expression for protein
>**Results** : Cell surface expression confirmed by FC. In WB, expression in cell lysates and supernatants with two bands for each. There is heavy glycosylation and treatment with an enzyme that cleave O-glycosylated (O-sialoglyco-protein) resulted in a lighter fragment. Supernatant form seemed clived, probably by metalloproteases. In FC using a rabbit antiserum, expression was confined to splenic and LN DC and higher in CD11c+. Activation of DC with LPS, anti-CD40 or poly(I:C) increased it's expression. By IHC, expression in spleen in white pulp, in LN, and in thymus in medulla. Increased by poly(I:C) injections.
>
>**Experiment** : Cell populations capable of binding CXCL16 by cloning a CXCL16-FC protein
>**Results** : Weak in T cells, but not on B cells, macrophages and neutrophils
>**Experiment** : Chemoattraction experiments
>**Results** : No attraction of resting T cells, B cells, macrophages or neutrophils. High attraction of activated CD8, and to a lesser degree, CD4 T cells. Lost when T cells are treated with pertussis toxin. Induces a calcium flux.
>
>**Experiment** : To find the ligand, library construction in HEK293 and binding to CXCL16-Fc, then sorting, amplification, several times.
>**Results** : Identification of a single protein with high homology to chemokine receptor (which was called Bonzo, and was identified as a HIV co-receptor). Verification by transfection and attraction assays.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > We describe a protein with the hallmarks of a chemokine, designated CXCL16, that is made by dendritic cells (DCs) in lymphoid organ T cell zones and by cells in the splenic red pulp. CXCL16 contains a transmembrane domain and both membrane-bound and soluble forms are produced. Naïve CD8 T cells, natural killer T cells and a subset of memory CD4 T cells bind CXCL16, and activated T cells migrated chemotactically to the soluble chemokine. By expression cloning, Bonzo (also known as STRL33 and TYMSTR) was identified as a CXCL16 receptor. CXCL16 may function in promoting interactions between DCs and CD8 T cells and in guiding T cell movements in the splenic red pulp. CXCL16 was also found in the thymic medulla and in some nonlymphoid tissues, indicating roles in thymocyte development and effector T cell trafficking.
