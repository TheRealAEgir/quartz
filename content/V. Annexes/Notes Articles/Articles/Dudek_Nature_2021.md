## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article described an auto-agressive CXCR6+PD1+ non-exhausted non-resident memory T cell population that cause liver damage in NASH mouse model. CXCR6 and PD1 expression depended on IL15 and FOXO1 downregulation. The hepatocyte killing was triggered by acetate exposure and ATP/NAD activation of P2RX7 but not TCR. Damage was caused by TNF and FAS. [[2. Hypothèses TIL]]
> 
>**Experiment** : Mouse under CD-HFD to induce NASH.
>**Results** : No NASH when there is no T cells. In NASH, T cells were CXCR6+(PD1+); exhausted, cytokine production and apoptosis, no proliferation. Not resident nor exhausted T cells. No BLIMP1, EOMES or TBET, but depended on the downregulated of FOXO1. FOXO1 inhibition/OE affect CXCR6 expression.
>
>**Other Results** : Higher serum IL15, IL21 and TNF concentration in NASH mice. In vitro treatment with IL15 induced CXCR6, PD1, GZMB and CD69 with FOXO1 downregulation. Correlation of liver CXCR6 with serum IL15. NASH mouse treated with anti-CD122 depleting antibody reduced CXCR6+PD1+ T cells and resulted in amelioration of disease.
>
>**Experiment** : Hepatocyte killing assay
>**Results** : No killing in normal conditions, but killing was induced by acetate exposure which resulted in higher GZMB and TNF secretion.
>**Other Results** : TNF inhibition lead to inhibition of auto-aggression and decrease in tissue damages, which was caused by decrease LFA1-ICAM1 interaction.
>
>**Experiment** : Anti-MHC-I or anti-PD1 
>**Results** : No effect. Activation was TCR independant, but purogenic receptor dependant (and mostly P2RX7). Validated from human T cells *ex vivo*.
>
>**Other Results** : FasL blockade limited auto-agression

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) is a manifestation of systemic metabolic disease related to obesity, and causes liver disease and cancer1,2. The accumulation of metabolites leads to cell stress and inflammation in the liver3, but mechanistic understandings of liver damage in NASH are incomplete. Here, using a preclinical mouse model that displays key features of human NASH (hereafter, NASH mice), we found an indispensable role for T cells in liver immunopathology. We detected the hepatic accumulation of CD8 T cells with phenotypes that combined tissue residency (CXCR6) with effector (granzyme) and exhaustion (PD1) characteristics. Liver CXCR6+ CD8 T cells were characterized by low activity of the FOXO1 transcription factor, and were abundant in NASH mice and in patients with NASH. Mechanistically, IL-15 induced FOXO1 downregulation and CXCR6 upregulation, which together rendered liver-resident CXCR6+ CD8 T cells susceptible to metabolic stimuli (including acetate and extracellular ATP) and collectively triggered auto-aggression. CXCR6+ CD8 T cells from the livers of NASH mice or of patients with NASH had similar transcriptional signatures, and showed auto-aggressive killing of cells in an MHC-class-I-independent fashion after signalling through P2X7 purinergic receptors. This killing by auto-aggressive CD8 T cells fundamentally differed from that by antigen-specific cells, which mechanistically distinguishes auto-aggressive and protective T cell immunity.
