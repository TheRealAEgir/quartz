> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article describe a Tex population that would latter be called Tpex in a 2019 review. It was described based on CXCR TFH-like expression, with all stem-like caracteristics compared to CXCR5-. Only this population responded to anti-PD-L1 treatment. FInaly, TCF1 is important to this population as TCF1Ko lymphocytes do not generate CXCR5+ T lymphocytes [[1.3 Dysfonction]]
> 
>**Experiment** : Study of CXCR5+ CD8+ T cells in LCMV infection
>**Results** : gp33+ CXCR5+ CD8+ T cells only exists in Cl13 but not Arm infection. Also ICOS+BCL6+TIM3- (lower ICP, higher co-stimulation). CXCR5+ resembled TFH with TCF1 expression while CXCR5- resembled Teff with BLIMP1, high granzymes but low TNF/IL2. CXCR5 had high EOMES/ID3, while CXCR5- had higher TBET/ID2.
>
>**Other Results** : CXCR5+ mostly in SLO (white pulp, T cell zone). Able to migrate to CXCL13, express CCR7 and XCL1. More proliferation, can give rise to CXCR5-TIM3+.
>
>**Experiment** : Adoptive transfer of sorted cells (high, and low numbers)
>**Results** : CXCR5+ is more stem like, proliferative and can differenciate into CXCR5- but not the opposite. Anti-PD-L1 antibody had more effect on CXCR5+.
>
>**Experiment** : WT of TCF1 KO and co-transfer into mouse and then infection.
>**Results** : No CXCR5+ generation inf the absence of TCF1.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Chronic viral infections are characterized by a state of CD8+ T-cell dysfunction that is associated with expression of the programmed cell death 1 (PD-1) inhibitory receptor–. A better understanding of the mechanisms that regulate CD8+ T cell responses during chronic infection is required to improve immunotherapies that restore function in exhausted CD8+ T cells. Here we identify a population of virus-specific CD8+ T cells that proliferate after blockade of the PD-1 inhibitory pathway in mice chronically infected with lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV). These LCMV-specific CD8+ T cells expressed the PD-1 inhibitory receptor but also expressed several costimulatory molecules such as ICOS and CD28. This CD8+ T cell subset was characterized by a unique gene signature that was related to that of CD4+ T follicular helper (TFH) cells, CD8+ T cell memory precursors and haematopoietic stem cell progenitors, but that was distinct from that of CD4+ TH1 cells and CD8+ terminal effectors. This CD8+ T cell population was found only in lymphoid tissues and resided predominantly in the T cell zones along with naïve CD8+ T cells. These PD-1+ CD8+ T cells resembled stem cells during chronic LCMV infection, undergoing self-renewal and also differentiating into the terminally exhausted CD8+ T cells that were present in both lymphoid and non-lymphoid tissues. The proliferative burst after PD-1 blockade came almost exclusively from this CD8+ T cell subset. Notably, the transcription factor TCF1 had a cell intrinsic and essential role in the generation of this CD8+ T cell subset. These findings provide a better understanding of T cell exhaustion and have implications in the optimization of PD-1-directed immunotherapy in chronic infections and cancer.
