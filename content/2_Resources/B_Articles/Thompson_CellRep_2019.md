## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article studied APC and TRM in anti-CD40/poly(IC:LC) vaccination in non-human primate, and found that anti-CD40/poly(IC:LC) stimulated blood monocyte contribute to TRM formation via IL-10 mediated secretion of TGFb. By comparing s.c. and i.v. vaccination of non-human primate with or without anti-CD40/poly(IC:LC), it was observed that i.v. induced a systemic response and that CD40 bound to macrophages/monocytes/neutrophils. Both vaccination strategy induced T cell response, but only stimulated i.v. induced CD103 expression, and this correlated with early systemic IL10 concentration. *In vitro* analyses revealed that blood monocytes but not DC were important for this upregulation, in a soluble factor. IL10 had a role, because blocking/addition confirmed the observation; however this role was not direct on T cells. IL10 seemed to induce secretion of membrane bound TGFb on monocyte. Finally, early TGFb exposure during T cell primed could have a role in TRM differenciation and CD103 upregulation, as non-TGFb exposed primed T cell had lower CD103 upregulation upon TGFb treatment. [[TRM Priming]] [[TRM Therapies]] [[TRM Differenciation]]
> 
>**Experiment** : i.v. or s.c. vaccination of non-human primate with anti-CD40 antibody and poly(IC:LC) (TLR3 agonist)
>**Results** : CD40 ab distributed through the body only with i.v. administration, and locally + dLN with s.c., were it bound macrophages, monocytes and neutrophils. There was robust T cells response in both, and they were detectable 9 month later. However, there was more antigen-specific CD103+ CD8+ T cells with i.v. Multivariate correlative analyses showed that 6 hours systemic IL10 had the highest correlation with formed TRM at 21 dpi. Both CD40 and poly(IC:LC) was important for it's secretion.
>
>**Experiment** : Sorting of blood/dermis/epidermis APC, stimulation with anti-CD40/poly(I:C) and co-culture with allogenic T cells
>**Results** : Only stimulated blood APC had the capacity to induce CD103 expression on CD8 T cells and secrete IL10. Role of IL10 was confirmed by blocking anti-IL10 anitbody and exogenous IL10 addition. However, addition of IL10 in CD3-CD28 stimulated T cells did not increase CD103, suggesting that IL10 target is not T cells.
>**Other Results** : APC produced TGFb upon stimulation, which was inhibited by anti-IL10 antibody and increased by exogenous IL10 treatment. Monocytes had higher membrane-bound TGFb, which decreased with stimulation in an anti-correlated way with increase in supernatant TGFb concentration, suggesting secretion.
>
>**Experiment** : Transwell of stimulated CD14+ monocytes (without DC) with CD3-CD28 stimulated T cells
>**Results** : Increase in CD103 expression, suggesting soluble factors involved.
>**Other Results** : Exogenous TGFb upregulated CD103 at higher rate when treating early.
>
>**Experiment** : Naive T cells treated with TGFb for 3 days, resting for 2 and sorting of CD103- T cells, then TGFb treatment.
>**Results** : CD103- upregulated CD103 upon TGFb exposure or without. However, T cell primed without TGFb upregulated CD103 to a lesser degree.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Using non-human primates (NHPs), mice, and human primary cells, we found a role for interleukin-10 (IL-10) in the upregulation of the tissue-resident memory T cell (TRM) marker CD103. In NHPs, intravenous, but not subcutaneous, immunization with peptide antigen and an adjuvant combining an agonistic anti-CD40 antibody plus poly(IC:LC) induced high levels of CD103+ TRMs in the lung, which correlated with early plasma IL-10 levels. Blocking IL-10 reduced CD103 expression on human T cells stimulated in vitro with the adjuvant combination as well as diminished CD103 on lung-resident T cells in vivo in mice. Monocyte-produced IL-10 induced the release of surface-bound transforming growth factor β (TGF-β), which in turn upregulated CD103 on T cells. Early TGF-β imprinted increased sensitivity to TGF-β restimulation, indicating an early commitment of the T cell lineage toward TRMs during the priming stage of activation. IL-10-mediated TGF-β signaling may therefore have a critical role in the generation of TRM following vaccination., , Thompson et al. outline a role for early IL-10 following immunization in the release of TGF-β and subsequent differentiation of CD103+ TRMs. Using a combination of non-human primate, human, and murine systems, the authors demonstrate that blocking IL-10 reduces CD103 expression, whereas delivery of IL-10 can augment a CD103+ phenotype.
