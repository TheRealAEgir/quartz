## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article studied memory T cells formation in intestine following infection. It describe that effector T cells intestine migration depend on chemokine receptor inhibited by pertussis toxin treatment, and that memory T cells cannot migrate to this tissue. There was a transient expression of alpha4beta7 integrin in effector and memory T cels, and reinfection of mouse lead to memory T cells migration in intestine. This activation seems to happened in spleen; there was more activation in spleen, other intestine-unrelated route of immunization resulted in similar intestinal T cells, and blocking T cells in GALT by FTY720 treatment still resulted in alpha4beta7 expressin in spleen effector T cells. Since mLN, iLN and spleen effector did not have the same alpha4beta7 and CCR9 expression, cells from these tissue were sorted and transfer, and it was shown that mLN had superior intestine migration potential, but that spleen had higher cell number, resulting in more intestine T cells. Cells from intestine seems to not recirculate; FTY720 treatment did not result in reduction of IEL cell number, and transplantation of intestine in immune mice resulted in more T cells in the transplant than in the donnor intestine mouse. Finally, similar dynamics could be found in human, where vaccination induced transient CLA and alpha4beta7 expression in tetramer+ specific T cells in skin. [[TRM Historic]]  [[TRM Priming]] [[TRM Migration]] [[TRM Precursors]] [[TRM Recirculation]]
> 
>**Experiment** : Transfer of naive Thy1.1 P14 T cells in naive Thy1.2 mouse and infectoin with LCVM Armstrong
>**Results** : There was no naive T cells in intestine IEL. Transfer of spleen effector resulted in T cells in intestine as well, which was inhibited by pertussis toxin treatment. Transfer of memory T cells did not resulted in something similar. Mixing of effector and memory showed that both are capable of trafficking to spleen, but effector are 50 time capable of trafficking to intestine, and memory 20 times to LN. Alpha4beta7 is transiently expressed on effector T cells. Reactivation of memory T cells with secondary infection resulted in reexpression of alpha4beta7 and dissemination in the intestine, but only when the secondary infection expressed the antigen. Activation was higher in spleen, suggesting this phenomenon happen in spleen and not intestine. This was confirmed by using intranasal and intramuscular infection, which also resulted in alpha4beta7 expression and migration into intestine, and by FTY720 GALT treatment which resulted in spleen alpha4beta7 effectors.
>
>**Other Results** : Alpha4beta7 and CCR9 expression is not maintained in memory T cells in lymphoid tissue, and intestine only express CCR9.
>
>**Experiment** : Isolation of effector T cells from iLN, spleen and mLN which expressed different levels of alpha4beta7 and CCR9, and transfer into separate recipient.
>**Results** : mLN cells were the most efficient at intestine homing, iLN were poor and spleen were intermediary. Since there is about 20 more spleen effector, it suggest that most intestine T cells came from spleen effector, and transfer of all spleen or all mLN T cells resulted in 2.5-4 time more intestine T cells in favor of spleen.
>
>**Experiment** : FTY720 treatment of immune mouse
>**Results** : There was an increase in iLN T cells, reduction in bood, but no reduction in IEL, suggesting no recirculation.
>
>**Experiment** : Transplantation of memory T cells-containing intestine into immune Thy1.1 mouse
>**Results** : mLN from donnor and recipient had equal number of memory T cells. Donor's IEL had kess memory T cells, indicating minimal recirculation.
>
>**Experiment** : Human vaccination and tetramer staining for effector/memory discrimination
>**Results** : Human T cells have similar CLA and alpha4beta7 transient expression in effector/memory specific T cells.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Migration to intestinal mucosa putatively depends on local activation because gastrointestinal lymphoid tissue induces expression of intestinal homing molecules, whereas skin-draining lymph nodes do not. This paradigm is difficult to reconcile with reports of intestinal T cell responses after alternative routes of immunization. We reconcile this discrepancy by demonstrating that activation within spleen results in intermediate induction of homing potential to the intestinal mucosa. We further demonstrate that memory T cells within small intestine epithelium do not routinely recirculate with memory T cells in other tissues, and we provide evidence that homing is similarly dynamic in humans after subcutaneous live yellow fever vaccine immunization. These data explain why systemic immunization routes induce local cell-mediated immunity within the intestine and indicate that this tissue must be seeded with memory T cell precursors shortly after activation.
