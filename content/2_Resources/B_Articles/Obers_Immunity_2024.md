>**Experiment** : WT or Tbet KO OT-I in mouse with LCMV-OVA infection
>**Results** : TRM in skin/liver were impaired, but SI was increased due to increased persistence. This was independent of CD103, with TBET/CD103 DKO being numerically unchanged.
>**Experiment** : Co-transfer of WT/TGFbRII KO or WT/TGFbRII-TBET DKO in mouse
>**Results** : Phenotype including CD103 was not affected by absence of TBET and TGFb signaling.
>**Experiment** : ATAC-seq of WT/TBET KO of SI TRM after LCMV-OVA infection
>**Results** : Increased BHLHE40/BLIMP1/FOS/CD103/CCR9 and decreased EOMES/S1PR1/CD62L. Increase in RA pathways and decrease in RA repressor HIC1.
>
>**Experiment** : Transfer of WT or TBET KO pre-treated or not with RA agonist/antagonist, or use of a dominant-negative form of RAR
>**Results** : RA activation lead to increase in TRM number in both WT and TBET KO, with higher increase in TBET KO. dnRARa abolished the increase of TBET over WT. Also, dnRARa P14 in LCMV model lead to decrease of CD127-KLRG1+ over CD127+KLRG1- in lymphoid tissue, reduced TRM precursors in liver/SI and decrease in TRM number in liver/kidney/SI, increase in skin and no change in LI.
>**Other Results** : HIC1 deletion did no alter SI TRM persistence, but impaired TRM formation in liver and SI but not in the skin.
>**Experiment** : Pre-treating of OT-I with RA and DNFB injection
>**Results** : Decrease in skin TRM, but increase in SI/liver.
>**Experiment** : RA agonist in mouse with WT/TGFbRII KO OT-I T cells
>**Results** : Increase of TGFbRII KO T cells in SI to a higher level than CCR9 OE, suggesting that it did not only increase migration.
>
>**Experiment** : RNAseq of TGFb or RA treated activated CD8 T cells and comparison with T cell populations signatures or with dnRARa isolated from mouse
>**Results** : TGFb treated ressembled skin/SI TRM while RA treated ressembled SI/liver TRM. RA induced genes included P2RX7, KLRC1, KLRK1, IL6RA, IL27RA, EVI5. dnRARa from SI/liver lost their SI/liver TRM signature and gained gene in the skin TRM singature (CCR8, CCR10) and T circ (SP1R1, S1PR4, CCR7). Combinaison of TGFb and RA showed similar set of genes that ressembled SI TRM, including AHR, CXCR6, RGS1 + OSGIN1, GPR34, GPR55, CISH and downregulation of Ly6C2, CD62L, TCF1, SLAMF6, CXCR4
>**Experiment** : *in vitro* treatment of T cells with RA and/or TGFb
>**Results** : RA drove CD69 and TGFb drove CD103 expression and they adopted a TRM-like phenotype.
>
>**Experiment** : (WT or CCR9 OE) dnRARa T cells
>**Results** : dnRARa were lost in the memory phase despite initial increase due to CCR9. Blocking RA signaling after 7 dpi resulted in decline of SI TRM.
>**Other Results** : There was no changes in BCL2/BIM and apoptosis
>**Other Results** : There was decrease in P2RX7
>**Other Results** : There was increase in tissue egress genes KL2/S1PR5/ZEB2
>**Experiment** : HOBIT fate mapping to track exTRM
>**Results** : There is less TRM in SI, more exTRM and more TRM + exTRM in mLN following RA antagonist treatment. FTY720 prevented this effect. This suggest that RA prevent TRM from exiting tissues.
>
>**Experiment** : Since microbiota can induce RA, use of dirty and SPF mouse.
>**Results** : Dirty mouse had more CD69+CD103+ TRM CD8 and CD4
>
>**Other Results** : dnRARa had higher secretion of IFNg and TNFa and RA agonist lead to reduced cytokine and dirty mouse TRM was similar.>

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Tissue-resident memory T (TRM) cells are integral to tissue immunity, persisting in diverse anatomical sites where they adhere to a common transcriptional framework. How these cells integrate distinct local cues to adopt the common TRM cell fate remains poorly understood. Here, we show that whereas skin TRM cells strictly require transforming growth factor β (TGF-β) for tissue residency, those in other locations utilize the metabolite retinoic acid (RA) to drive an alternative differentiation pathway, directing a TGF-β-independent tissue residency program in the liver and synergizing with TGF-β to drive TRM cells in the small intestine. We found that RA was required for the long-term maintenance of intestinal TRM populations, in part by impeding their retrograde migration. Moreover, enhanced RA signaling modulated TRM cell phenotype and function, a phenomenon mirrored in mice with increased microbial diversity. Together, our findings reveal RA as a fundamental component of the host-environment interaction that directs immunosurveillance in tissues.
