## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article assessed metabolism in TRM and found that TRM rely on FABP4 and FABP5 for lipid metabolism and their maintenance. Transcriptionnal analysis showed that TRM expressed FABP4/FABP5/CD36/PPARG compaired to TCM and that they had higher lipid uptake. FABP4/FABP5 DKO cells showed reduced number of TRM in the skin starting at 25 dpi and lipid uptake was reduced. PPARG KO or inhibition had similar results, and reduced FABP4/5. Seahorse experiment confirmed FA metabolism. Inhibition of beta-oxydation with etomoxir/trimetazidine or CPT1A KO had similar effects to the DKO. This effected viral clearance and protection against re-challenge with lethal doses, and IFNg was not implicated. FABP4/5 could be found in human sample of psioriasis infected skin, and these cell had lipid uptake *ex vivo*. [[TRM Phenotype]] [[TRM Caracteristics]] [[TRM Caracteristics]] 
> 
>**Experiment** : Transfer of OT-I cells in mouse and infection with VAVC-OVA + transcriptionnal profiling
>**Results** : TRM in the skin from 5 dpi, increasing until 10 dpi. Transcriptome from 25 dpi and 90 dpi are similar. In comparison to TCM, TRM expressed FABP4/FABP5/CD36. PPARG as well, and it was confirmed that this was an upstream regulator of FABP4/FABP5
>
>**Experiment** : Lipid uptake measurement by OT-I T cells using fluorescent lipid bodipy FL C16
>**Results** : *In vitro* OT-I TRM cells had more lipid uptake than TN/TCM/TEM. FABP4/FABP5 DKO had less lipid uptake *in vivo*.
>
>**Experiment** : Co-transfer of WT/FABP4/FABP5 DKO OT-I T cells in mouse then infection with VACV-OVA.
>**Results** : Same number of spleen TCM. Decline of DKO TRM in skin starting from 25 dpi. FABP4 or FABP5 single KO was not affected, suggesting that the two protein can compensate each other. Similar result when PPARG KO or inhibited using GW9662 pharmalogical inhibitor. DKO cells had higher apoptosis.
>
>**Experiment** : Seahorse
>**Results** : Extracellular FA resulted in higher oxyen consumption rate (OCR), that was blocked by etomixr (inhibitor of mitochondrial fatty acid beta-oxidation enzyme carnitine palmitoyltransferase). This wasn't observed in DKO cells.
>**Other Results** : *in vivo* treatement with etomoxir or trimetazidine or CPT1A KO resulted in similar TRM reduction than DKO.
>
>**Experiment** : BM mixing of WT/ FABP4/FABP5 DKO in mice, then infection with rVACV (no OT-I and OVA) + re-challenge with FTY720
>**Results** : There was less DKO TRM cells and were less efficient in viral clearance. Etomoxir treatement had similar result on WT than the untreated DKO condition. There was no differences in IFNg production.
>
>**Experiment** : RAG1-/- mouse reconstitued with CD4 and CD8 WT or DKO and rVACV infection in the skin, then intranasal re-challenge with lethal doses or WR-VACV
>**Results** : Mouse with DKO was only partially protected compaired to WT control and FTY720 treatement reuced this result.
>
>**Experiment** : Human confirmation with psioriasis region skin sample
>**Results** : There was TRM that expressed FABP4 and FABP5, and they could uptake lipid *ex vivo*.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Tissue-resident memory T (TRM) cells persist indefinitely in epithelial barrier tissues and protect the host against pathogens–. However, the biological pathways that enable the long-term survival of TRM cells are obscure,. Here we show that mouse CD8+ TRM cells generated by viral infection of the skin differentially express high levels of several molecules that mediate lipid uptake and intracellular transport, including fatty-acid-binding proteins 4 and 5 (FABP4 and FABP5). We further show that T-cell-specific deficiency of Fabp4 and Fabp5 (Fabp4/Fabp5) impairs exogenous free fatty acid (FFA) uptake by CD8+ TRM cells and greatly reduces their long-term survival in vivo, while having no effect on the survival of central memory T (TCM) cells in lymph nodes. In vitro, CD8+ TRM cells, but not CD8+ TCM, demonstrated increased mitochondrial oxidative metabolism in the presence of exogenous FFAs; this increase was not seen in Fabp4/Fabp5 double-knockout CD8+ TRM cells. The persistence of CD8+ TRM cells in the skin was strongly diminished by inhibition of mitochondrial FFA β-oxidation in vivo. Moreover, skin CD8+ TRM cells that lacked Fabp4/Fabp5 were less effective at protecting mice from cutaneous viral infection, and lung Fabp4/Fabp5 double-knockout CD8+ TRM cells generated by skin vaccinia virus (VACV) infection were less effective at protecting mice from a lethal pulmonary challenge with VACV. Consistent with the mouse data, increased FABP4 and FABP5 expression and enhanced extracellular FFA uptake were also demonstrated in human CD8+ TRM cells in normal and psoriatic skin. These results suggest that FABP4 and FABP5 have a critical role in the maintenance, longevity and function of CD8+ TRM cells, and suggest that CD8+ TRM cells use exogenous FFAs and their oxidative metabolism to persist in tissue and to mediate protective immunity.
