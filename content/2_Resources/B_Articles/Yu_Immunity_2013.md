## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article used humanised mouse and showed that the CD1c+ but not the CD141+ lung DC population are capable of inducing TGFb dependant CD103 expression on T cells. These two DC population were observed in human lung, and were put in a mouse with CD34+ reconstitution and LIAV injection. Both induced similar antigen-specific T cells expansion and cytotoxicity in autologous/allogenic, *in vivo*/*in vitro* models. CD1c+ DC were capable of inducing CD103 expression in CD103- T cells, and these cells had higher residency in *in vitro* models or *in vivo* human E-cadherin+ lung graft in a CD103 dependant manner. Despite similar TGFb transcript, only CD1c+ had membrane bound active TGFb1 expression, and this could be due to expression of TGFb activating proteins. Finally, the CD103 induction was TGFb dependant. [[TRM Differenciation]] [[TRM Caracteristics]] [[TRM Differenciation]]
> 
>**Results** : in human lung, there is CD1c+ and CD141+ DC populations. CD1c had higher HLA-DR and CD86. Localisation was different.
>
>**Experiment** : Humanized mouse model by transfer of CD34+ HPC, and i.n. LIAV injection
>**Results** : Similar amount of CD1c+ and CD141+ DC in the lung. The to population captured antigen and induced expansion of antigen-specific FluM1 CD8+ T cells.
>
>**Experiment** : Isolation of CD1c+ or CD141+ DC and allogenic or autologous co-culture with T cells from humanized mouse or human
>**Results** : CD141+ induced higher proliferation. Looking at T cell phenotype, CD1c+ resulted in higher CD103+ T cells. This was the result of CD103 induction as CD1c were capable of inducing CD103 expression on purified CD103- T cells. Both DC educated T cells into a cytotoxic state (GZMA+GZMB+Perforine+) and they were able to kill peptide-pulsed T2 cells.
>
>**Experiment** : Stamper-Woodruff tissue biding assay
>**Results** : CD1c+ educated T cells had higher residency, and blocking CD103 decreased this effect.
>**Experiment** : Engraftement of human E-cadherin+ epithelial cells in mouse lung, and injection of CD1c+ or CD141+ DC educated T cells in the graft.
>**Results** : T cells educated by CD1c+ DC were more resident into the graft, and blocking CD103 decreased this effect. These T cells showed limited proliferation, suggesting residence instead of proliferation.
>**Experiment** : Reconstitution of mouse with E-cadherin lung graft, CD1c+ or CD141+ blood DC and CFSE labelled T cells
>**Results** : Only mouse reconstitued with CD1c+ DC had T cells in the graft, which proliferated and acquired CD103. There was the same amount of T cells in spleen in both DC conditions.
>
>**Experiment** : Blockade of GM-CSF
>**Results** : No impact of CD1c+ DC derived CD103 expression on T cells.
>**Other Results** : CD1c+ DC had membrane bound TGFb1 expression. The two DC subset had similar TGFb transcript, but CD1c+ had higher expression of TGFb activation protein (furin, MMP9, CD36, PLAUR)
>
>**Experiment** : Blocking of TGFb, inhibition of TGFbRI kinase or adding exogenous CD103
>**Results** : All resulted in CD103 expression loss/gain, validating the implication of TGFb in CD1c+ DC induced CD103 expression and the inability of CD141+ DC to induce CD103 expression.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > In comparison to murine dendritic cells (DCs), less is known about the function of human DCs in tissues. Here, we analyzed, using lung tissues from humans and humanized mice, the role of human CD1c+ and CD141+ DCs in determining the type of CD8+ T cell immunity generated to live-attenuated influenza virus (LAIV) vaccine. We found that both lung DC subsets acquired influenza antigens in vivo and expanded specific cytotoxic CD8+ T cells in vitro. However, lung-tissue-resident CD1c+ DCs but not CD141+ DCs were able to drive CD103 expression on CD8+ T cells and promoted CD8+ T cell accumulation in lung epithelia in vitro and in vivo. CD1c+ DCs induction of CD103 expression was dependent on membrane-bound cytokine TGF-β1. Thus, CD1c+ and CD141+ DCs generate CD8+ T cells with different properties, and CD1c+ DCs specialize in the regulation of mucosal CD8+ T cells.
