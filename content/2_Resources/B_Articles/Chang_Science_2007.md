## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : [[1.1 Différenciation des lymphocytes T en lymphocyte T effecteur ou mémoire]]
> 
>**Experiment** : Transfer of P14 into mouse and infection with gp33-LM, and examination of CFSE undivided T cell by microscopy.
>**Results** : There was a polarisation of the MTOC with CD3/CD8 and LFA1 tower the membrane. When cells were in anaphase, there as one MTOC at the other end of the cell. Also in CD4 with CD3/CD4/IFNgR. PKC was localized at the other end.
>**Experiment** : T cells into RAG KO mouse, to observe HP, or ICAM1 KO to disable LFA-1 dependant interaction with DC.
>**Results** : There was proliferation, but no assymetric cell division.
>
>**Experiment** : Blocking daughter cell division with cytochalasin B
>**Results** : There was assymetric distribution of CD8 and PKC
>Also, difference in IFNgR and Numb could explain different fate. There was difference in CD62L/CD69/CD43/CD44/CD25 expression, suggesting effector and memory phenotype.
>
>**Experiment** : Sorting and transfer of distal and proximal T cell into mouse, and re-challenge at effector or memory timepoint
>**Results** : Distal are better and memory timepoint while proximal are better at effector timepoint.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > A hallmark of mammalian immunity is the heterogeneity of cell fate that exists among pathogen-experienced lymphocytes. We show that a dividing T lymphocyte initially responding to a microbe exhibits unequal partitioning of proteins that mediate signaling, cell fate specification, and asymmetric cell division. Asymmetric segregation of determinants appears to be coordinated by prolonged interaction between the T cell and its antigen-presenting cell before division. Additionally, the first two daughter T cells displayed phenotypic and functional indicators of being differentially fated toward effector and memory lineages. These results suggest a mechanism by which a single lymphocyte can apportion diverse cell fates necessary for adaptive immunity.
