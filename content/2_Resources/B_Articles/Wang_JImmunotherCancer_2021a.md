> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : After observing that tumor CD8+ T cells had higher CXCR6 expression and that CXCR6 expressing T cells in auto-immune disease had highly functionnal, this paper describe a highly functionnal CXCR6+ CD8+ T cell population in mouse tumor. In CXCR6 CD8 conditionnal KO mouse, tumor growth is higher, and CD8+ T cells are less functionnal. Similar results are found with anti-CXCR6 depleting antibodies. Anti-CD40 greatly increase CXCR6+ T cell population in the tumor. Anti-PD1 efficiency is higher in WT mouse. When co-transfering WT/KO T cells in mouse, there is mouse WT in tumor but equal amount everywhere else. CXCR6+ are more numerous in MSI colon cancer vs MSS. CXCL16 expression is constant in tumors. FACS sorted CXCR6+ vs CXCR6- bulk RNA seq showed a more differenciated phenotype of CXCR6+ T cells. [[TRM Cancer]] [[TRM Cancer]] [[Output/Protéine/CXCR6]]
> 
>**Experiment** : analyses of pubblicly available scRNAseq datasets
>**Results** : CXCR6 is expressed by (CD8+) T cells and enriched in tumor, validated by IHC and in mouse model. CXCR6 was expressed on cytotoxic T cell cluster and CXCR6 expression was correlated with TCM infiltration.
>
>**Experiment** : CXCR6 CD8 conditionnal KO mouse and tumor innoculation
>**Results** : lower survival and higher tumor growth in KO mouse with less CD8 T cell tumor infiltration. CXCR6 KO CD8+ T cells had lower IFNg, perforine 1 and TNFa secretion. Tumor progression was higher in WT mouse treated with depleting anti-CXCR6 antibodies.
>
>**Experiment** : anti-CD40 and anti-PD1 treatment in mouse with tumors
>**Results** : Increase of the CXCR6 T cell population. Anti-PD1 efficiency was decreased in CXCR6 KO mouse. CXCR6+ T cells had higher cytokine secretion.
>
>**Experiment** : Co-transfer of WT or CXCR6 KO T cells in mouse with tumor
>**Results** : Similar proportion except in tumor were there was more WT cells with more cytokine production.
>
>**Other Results** : CXCR6 T cells were more numerous in MSI vs MSS colon cancer.
>
>**Experiment** : Bulk RNAseq of FACS sorted CXCR6- vs CXCR6+ CD8+ T cells.
>**Results** : More effector, activation, chemokines/receptors, cytokines/receptor in CXCR6+ with more terminally differenciated state.
>
>**Other Results** : There was no changes in CXCL16 expression in tumor.
>**Other Results** : CXCR6 expression is increase in the TME, but co-culture with tumor cell is not sufficient.
>
>**Experiment** : Transfer of CXCR6+ vs CXCR6- CD8+ T cells in tumor bearing mouse + CXCR6 KO OT-I
>**Results** : Tumor progression was lower in CXCR6+ injected mouse. CXCR6 KO OT-I cells were weaker in antitumor activity.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Background
Increasing infiltration of CD8+ T cells within tumor tissue predicts a better prognosis and is essential for response to checkpoint blocking therapy. Furthermore, current clinical protocols use unfractioned T cell populations as the starting point for transduction of chimeric antigen receptors (CARs)-modified T cells, but the optimal T cell subtype of CAR-modified T cells remains unclear. Thus, accurately identifying a group of cytotoxic T lymphocytes with high antitumor efficacy is imperative. Inspired by the theory of yin and yang, we explored a subset of CD8+ T cell in cancer with the same phenotypic characteristics as highly activated inflammatory T cells in autoimmune diseases.

Methods
Combination of single-cell RNA sequencing, general transcriptome sequencing data and multiparametric cytometric techniques allowed us to map CXCR6 expression on specific cell type and tissue. We applied Cxcr6−/− mice, immune checkpoint therapies and bone marrow chimeras to identify the function of CXCR6+CD8+ T cells. Transgenic Cxcr6−/− OT-I mice were employed to explore the functional role of CXCR6 in antigen-specific antitumor response.

Results
We identified that CXCR6 was exclusively expressed on intratumoral CD8+ T cell. CXCR6+CD8+ T cells were more immunocompetent, and chimeras with specific deficiency on CD8+ T cells showed weaker antitumor activity. In addition, Cxcr6−/− mice could not respond to anti-PD-1 treatment effectively. High tumor expression of CXCR6 was not mainly caused by ligand-receptor chemotaxis of CXCL16/CXCR6 but induced by tumor tissue self. Induced CXCR6+CD8+ T cells possessed tumor antigen specificity and could enhance the effect of anti-PD-1 blockade to retard tumor progression.

Conclusions
This study may contribute to the rational design of combined immunotherapy. Alternatively, CXCR6 may be used as a biomarker for effective CD8+ T cell state before adoptive cell therapy, providing a basis for tumor immunotherapy.
