## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This paper describe increase in APC (DC and macrophages) in skin following inflammation (HSV1 infection or DNFB injection). This population peaked at 7 days, and never return to naive numbers after contraction. It express CCR2, and CCR2 KO mouse had less increase in similar conditions, suggesting a role for this chemokine receptor. These cells showed BrdU uptake, suggesting that they come from proliferating precursor. When transfering gDT-II at the same time of a HSV1 infection in skin with previous inflammation, CCR2 KO mouse had less gDT-II IFNg+ cells, suggesting a role for these APC in CD4 activation. Similarly, removing CD11c+ APC in CD11c.DTR transgenic mouse resulted in similar IFNg+ gDT-II decrease. [[TRM Differenciation]]
> 
>**Experiment** : HSV-1 skin infection
>**Results** : There was CD11ChiMHC II+CD64low DC and CD11Clow/int MHC II + CD64hi macrophages in the skin following infection, only on the flank that was infected, that peak at about 30-50 fold at 7 dpi. Even after contraction, there still was 2-4 fold more than naive skin. There was an accumulation at hair follicles. In the naive skin, there also was a transient accumulation following infection on the other flank, but it was less profound and did return to the same level than before after infection resolution.
>**Experiment** : DNFB infection
>**Results** : Although not to the same extend, there was the same observation with DNFB than with HSV-1 infected skin.
>
>**Other Results** : DC and macrophages expressed CCR2, and it is known than CCR2 monocytes are recruited through CCR2/CCL2 axis. CCR2 KO mouse had less increase following inflammation than WT.
>
>**Other Results** : In skin at memory timepoint, APC had BrdU uptake, suggesting that they came from precursors (as Langerhans cells does).
>
>**Experiment** : Generation of skin memory on WT CCR2 KO, then infection with HSV1 and tranfer of gDT-II CD4 T cells.
>**Results** : There was less IFNg+ gDT-II cells in the skin but not in the dLN (suggesting a localized response when APC are on the site of infection)
>
>**Experiment** : Use od CD11c.DTR mouse in which diphteria toxin treatment result in the depletion of CD11c expreesing cells, then infection with HSV1 and transfer of gDT-II CD4 T cells
>**Results** : The absence of APC resulted in decrease of IFNg+ gDT-II cells in the skin.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Antigen-presenting cells (APC), such as dendritic cells (DC) and macrophages, are critical for T-cell-mediated immunity. Although it is established that memory T cells accumulate and persist in peripheral tissues after the resolution of infection, whether this is also the case for APC remains unclear. Here, we report that CCR2-dependent cells infiltrate skin during acute infection with herpes simplex virus (HSV)-1 and subsequently give rise to localized populations of DCs and macrophages. These APC are found at elevated numbers at sites of resolved infection or inflammation compared with unaffected regions of skin. Importantly, this local accumulation of APC is sustained for prolonged periods of time and has important functional consequences, as it promotes interferon-γ responses by virus-specific CD4+ T cells upon localized challenge infection with HSV-1. Thus, our results highlight how infection history determines long-term changes in immune cell composition in skin and how different types of immune cells accumulate, persist and co-operate to provide optimal immunity at this critical barrier site.
