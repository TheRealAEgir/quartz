## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article defined a strict methdology to define [[dendritic cells]] classification using scRNAseq,  unsupervised FC and bulk-RNAseq. Then, they defined a DC3 population in breast cancer (CD88-CD1c+CD163+CD14+/-), and found that it ressembled iDC. cDC2 depended on FTL3L and DC3 on GM-CSF. cDC2 did no trans-differenciate into DC3, but DC3 did upregulate CD14 upon FLT3L. There seem to exist some precursor for this population. Co-culture of TLR-activated cDC2/DC3 with naive CD45RA+ allogenic T cells resulted in expansion and acquisition of CD45RO, but only with DC3 there was a TGFb-dependant CD103 upregulation in T cells. Sorted CD103+ T cells in this context did ressembled TRM, and there was a positive correlation between DC3 and CD69+CD103+ TRM infiltration in breast cancer. [[TRM Priming]] [[TRM Differenciation]]
> 
>**Experiment** : scRNAseq of CD16-CD141-CD123- phagocytes from blood
>**Results** : There was 4 distincts clusters, with A and B ressembing cDC2 and CD and D ressembling CD14+ monocytes/DC3. FC unsupervised analysis from scRNAseq-based markers recapitulated the data, and made the gating strategy : monocytes (CD88+CD14+), cDC2 (CD5+ and CD5-CD88-CD1c+BTLA+CD163-) and DC3 (CD88-CD1c+BTLA-CD163+). This was validated with bulk RNAseq of sorted populations. Seemed focused on DC3.
>
>**Experiment** : FC analyses of luminal BC phagocytes
>**Results** : Validation of gating strategy. There was no association of DC3 with disease progression, and they were as abundant as cDC2 and more than cDC1. cDC1 infiltration correlated with cDC2 infiltration.
>**Experiment** : bulk RNAseq of LN sorted CD1c+CD14+ iDC
>**Results** : Ressembled blood DC3, and so DC3 ressemble iDC in breast cancer.
>
>**Experiment** : Humanized mouse model with GM-CSF or FLT3L constitutive cell line
>**Results** : There was more cDC2 in FLT3L mouse and more DC3 in GM-CSF mouse. This was also the case with co-culture of human CD34+ confirmed with scRNAseq.
>
>**Experiment** : Trans-differenciation of cDC2 and DC3
>**Results** : sorted cDC2 did not showed trans-differenciation in GM-CSF co-culture or mouse with GM-CSF tumors, but DC3 did upregulate CD14.
>
>**Experiment** : Sorted CDP, cMoP and GMDP (precursors) and co-culture with GM-CSF/FTL3L cell lines.
>**Results** : In control conditions, expected results with CDP and cMoP. GMDP gave rise to macro/cDC2/Granulocytes with control cell line, and also DC3 with GM-CSF cell line.
>**Experiment** : Same but with soluble GM-CSF (with justifications)
>**Results** : Most progenitors had unipotent fate and gave rise to only one cell type. In most case, the DC3-forming progenitors were the one with the most pluripotent fate; however, there seemed to exist a unipotent DC3 cell line.
>
>**Experiment** : Activation of sorted DC3, cDC2 and monocyte with TLR agonist cocktail.
>**Results** : Bulk RNAseq showed a transcriptome convergence and upregulation of activation induced genes after a night, but they were still distinct.
>
>**Experiment** : Co-culture of DC3, cDC2 and monocytes TLR activated with allogenic CD45RA+ naive T cells and synthetic superantigen.
>**Results** : DC3 and cDC2 induced the most expansion and CD45RO acquisition. (also with *in vitro* differenciated DC3). T cells primed with DC3 and cDC2 showed TNFa and IFNg but not IL17 secretion. CD103 was upregulated in T cells when using DC3, even without antigen, and this was dependent on TGFb as neutralizing antibody aborted the oservation. CD103+ ressembled TRM with NUSAP1/DUSP4/CXCR6/FASLG/PRF1/GZMA expression
>**Other Results** : There was a positive correlation in DC3 and CD69+CD103+ infiltration in human BC samples (and not with other phagocytes).

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Dendritic cells (DCs) are antigen-presenting cells controlling T cell activation. In humans, the diversity, ontogeny, and functional capabilities of DC subsets are not fully understood. Here, we identified circulating CD88−CD1c+CD163+ DCs (called DC3s) as immediate precursors of inflammatory CD88−CD14+CD1c+CD163+FcεRI+ DCs. DC3s develop via a specific pathway activated by GM-CSF, independent of cDC-restricted (CDP) and monocyte-restricted (cMoP) progenitors. Like classical DCs but unlike monocytes, DC3s drove activation of naive T cells. In vitro, DC3s displayed a distinctive ability to prime CD8+ T cells expressing a tissue homing signature and the epithelial homing alpha-E integrin (CD103) through transforming growth factor β (TGF-β) signaling. In vivo, DC3s infiltrated luminal breast cancer primary tumors, and DC3 infiltration correlated positively with CD8+CD103+CD69+ tissue-resident memory T cells. Together, these findings define DC3s as a lineage of inflammatory DCs endowed with a strong potential to regulate tumor immunity., •DC3s are phenotypic and functional intermediates between cDC2s and monocytes•GM-CSF alone, but not FLT3L, supports efficient differentiation of DC3s•DC3s do not differentiate via cDC (CDP)- or monocyte-restricted (cMoP) progenitors•DC3s prime TRM cells in vitro and correlate with TRM expansion in primary breast cancer, Bourdely et al. identify human CD88−CD1c+CD163+ DC3s as a pro-inflammatory phagocyte lineage sharing features with monocytes and conventional DCs. DC3s efficiently induce differentiation of CD103+CD8+ T cells in vitro, and their infiltration correlates with CD8+CD69+CD103+ TRM accumulation in breast tumors.
