## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article describe TRM and TEX generated via LCMV Arm and Cl13 and showed that, despite being similar in residency protein expression, these two population are different in cytokine production, and expression of other genes. The two population depended on BLIMP1, RUNX3 and in some tissue to HOBIT, but only TEX depended on TOX. TRM could differenciate into TEX after re-transfer and challenge with LCMV Cl13, but Tex did not acquire residency program with similar experiment and challenge with LCMV Arm (but it did with challenge with another Cl13). Then, there was the description of a TRM and TEX specific signature and membrane protein marker (CD73 for TRM and CD200R for TEX). The use of these signature showed that high TRM signature in CD8 hi patient had higher survival, but only the TEX signature predicted ICB response. This was confirmed in in vivo experiment where only TEX responded to the treatment.
> [[TRM Phenotype]] [[TRM Heterogeneity]] [[TRM Differenciation]] [[TRM Cancer]] [[Exhaustion]] [[TRM Immunotherapy]] [[TRM Prognosis]] 
> 
>**Experiment** : Generation of TRM (CD69+CD103+ or CD69+CXCR6+ depending on tissue site) and TEX with LCMV Arm and Cl13 infection and P14 T cells.
>**Results** : Tex expressed TRM associated markers and TRM expressed Tex associated markers (lower expression) in a similar kinetic manner. There was lower cytokine production in Tex-term
>**Experiment** : Transfer of TRM and Tex in naive mouse and infection with LCMV Arm
>**Results** : Higher proliferation and survival of TRM.
>
>**Experiment** : Protein-RNA-ATACseq of Arm/Cl13 from various tissue
>**Results** : Cluster annotation. PCA showed similarity of TRM from liver/SI/SG and heterogeneity to Cl13 and TCIRCM. There was common DEG of TRM vs TCIRC and TRM vs Tex, including CD103/FOX/RUNX3/KLF/TCF1/CCR7. There was DEG relative to tissue and independant to TRM/TEX. TRM had higher TCF1 and Tex higher TOX/PD1/CD39. With motifs analyses, higher REL/NFkB and TCF/LEF in TRM and NR4A/NFAT/IRF in Tex. Also different regarding regulons.
>
>**Experiment** : Co-transfer of WT or (RUNX3 or BLIMP1 or HOBIT or TOX) KO T cells in naive mouse, then LCMV Arm and Cl13 infection.
>**Results** : BLIMP1 and RUNX3 are important for TRM and TEX. HOBIT varied between tissues. However, TOX deletion only affect TEX formation.
>
>**Experiment** : sort-rechallenge of TRM and TEX (divided in Tex-prog/Tex-int/Tex-term)
>**Results** : TRM can exhaust after Cl13 infection. Tex arose from TRM retained caracteristics from TRM, including CXCR6/CD49a expression. When doing the opposite, Tex-prog formed resident T cells in tissue but failed to express CD69/CD103/CXCR6/CD49a/CD73/P2RX7 and expressed Ly6C/CX3CR1 with Arm but not Cl13 infection.
>
>**Other Results** : Generation of a TRM and TEX signature. CD73 was the gene most uniquely expressed in TRM and CD200R in TEX.
>
>**Other Results** : Signature in bulk RNA of cancer patient showed that TR-TEX signature had a predictive value while TRM did not in CD8hi patients in metastatic melanoma, anti-PD1 treated urothelial cancer and anti-PD1/CTLA4 metastatic melanoma, but not with survival in metabrix where TRM are better. In vivo experiment showed only TR-TEX respond to ICB.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > The presence of CD8+ T cells coexpressing residency and exhaustion molecules in chronic diseases often correlate with clinical outcomes; however, the relationship between these cells and conventional tissue-resident memory (TRM) cells or exhausted CD8+ T (TEX) cells is unclear. Here we show that chronic antigen stimulation drives development of tissue-resident TEX (TR-TEX) cells that are distinct from TRM cells generated after antigen clearance. TR-TEX and TRM cells are regulated by different transcriptional networks with only TR-TEX cells being Tox-dependent for residency programming. While TEX cells (including TR-TEX) are unable to generate TRM cells after antigen withdrawal, TRM cells differentiate into TEX cells upon chronic antigen exposure. Cell-state-specific transcriptional signatures reveal a selective association of TR-TEX cells with patient responses to immune checkpoint blockade, and only TR-TEX but not TRM cells responded to PD-1 pathway inhibition in vivo. These data suggest that TR-TEX and TRM cells are developmentally divergent cell states that share a tissue-residency program but have distinct roles in disease control.
