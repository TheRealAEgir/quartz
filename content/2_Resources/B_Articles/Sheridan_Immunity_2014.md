## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%
> 
> **Summary** : This article talked about the [[TRM Differenciation]] in intestine epithelium in a model of oral infection with L. monocytogenes which was doing effective protection. This population is induced by rapid accumulation of CD103 expressing MPEC. This observation maybe explained by apoptosis of SLEC by TGFb while this cytokine upregulate MPEC's CD103. Other infection settings induced SLEC in intestine, which lead to decreased memory formation. [[TRM Precursors]]  [[TRM Therapies]] [[TRM Phenotype]] [[TRM Caracteristics]] [[TRM Infection]]
> 
>**Experiment** : Oral infection with L. Monocytogenes
>**Model** : L. Monocytogenes recognise E-cadherhin through internalin A, but differences in human vs mouse E-cadherin made previous model biased. Here, they used a recombinant bacteria taht correctly recognise E-cadherin. Also, the mouse has antigen-specific CD8 T cells.
>**Results** : Detection of the population in the blood 6-7 dpi with peak at 9, which wasn't impacted by the removal of the spleen, and evidence of priming in mesenteric LN.
>**Model** : Treatment with DATK-32 (anti alpha4beta7), which block intestinal mucosa migration.
>**Results** : There was less Trm in the intestinal epithelium when all other T cells population was not affected, and this resulted in higher bacteria burden.
>
>**Other Results** : At 12 dpi, antigen-specific T cells in the spleen are 50% KLRG1+ and 20% CD127+. In the intestinal, almost all cells are KLRG1-CD127+ and cytokine multifunctionnal.
>
>
>**Experiment** : To understand why there was more MPEC in the intestine and SLEC in the spleen, mice where treated daily with DATK-32 between 7-11 dpi
>**Results** : Reduced antigen-specific population suggested that alhpha4beta7 was important and that there was migration of T cells during this period. There was no alteration in the MPEC observation. There was still no alteration even with treatment with DATK-32 or anti-CCR9 from the beginning of the primary infection. This accumulation seemed caused by the apoptosis of SLEC which displayed high annexin V rather than MPEC proliferation which displayed low Brdu incorporation.
>
>**Other Results** : MLN and intestinal MPEC expressed CD103, contrary to spleen MPEC or SLEC. CD69 Was expressed by MPEC.
>
>**Experiment** : I.N. infection of mice with ovalbumin-epxressing influenza.
>**Results** : This model resulted in intestine largely made of SLEC and EEC. There was almost no ova-specific T cells at 25 dpi, but MPEC where still CD103. Same with i.n. L. monocytogenes infection.
>
>**Experiment** : Co-transfer of WT and CD103-/- T cells and oral L. monocytogenes infection.
>**Results** : Comparable ratios at the peak of T cells response in MLN and LP, but epthelium were WT, suggesting for a role of CD103 in epithelium accumulation, in an antigen-independent manner. This was not the case long-term, leading to the conclusion that is was not required for the rapid MPEC development.
>
>**Experiment** : Co-transfer of WT and TFGbRII cre
>**Results** : Ratios comparable in lymhoid tissue and instestinal at the peak of the response and in the memory phase. However, LP and IEL were barely detectable at 16 and 32 dpi with no CD103 expression. CD69 is independent of TGFb signaling.
>
>**Other Results** : TGFbRII KO showed a SLEC phenotype and did not form memory population.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > The intestinal mucosa promotes T cell responses that may be beneficial for effective mucosal vaccines. However, intestinal resident memory T (Trm) cell formation and function are poorly understood. We found that oral infection with Listeria monocytogenes induced a robust intestinal CD8 T cell response and blocking effector T cell migration showed that intestinal Trm cells were critical for secondary protection. Intestinal effector CD8 T cells were predominately composed of memory precursor effector cells (MPEC) that rapidly upregulated CD103, which was needed for T cell accumulation in the intestinal epithelium. CD103 expression, rapid MPEC formation, and maintenance in intestinal tissues were dependent on T cell intrinsic transforming growth factor-β signals. Moreover, intestinal Trm cells generated after intranasal or intravenous infection were less robust and phenotypically distinct from Trm cells generated after oral infection demonstrating the critical contribution of infection route for directing the generation of protective intestinal Trm cells.
