## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article used rigorous T cell quantification through a IF microscopy technique, and found that regular T cells isolation technique from NLT underestimate TRM cell compartiment. Flow cytometry underestimated memory T cells by a factor 50-70 in musocal tissues, 6-27 in other NLT and 2 in spleen. Isolation also overestimate blood/marginated cells over tissue, epithelium over LP, and CD103 expression. Using parabiosis for true residency, it was found that most instravascular injection-unstained cells are actually resident, and most memory P14 T cells were TRM. Half of the memory pool was in SLO, and blood only contained 4%. Thymus and medulla was permissive to memory T cells recirculation. In kidney, even marginated pool was actually TRM. Among resident T cells, only 25-75 actually expressed CD69. There was CD69- TEM P14 memory T cells that egress SG and FRT. Some marginal TRM expressed CD69. Finally, TEM and TCM had the same recruitement capacity after re-challenge. [[TRM Historic]] [[TRM Caracteristics]] [[Method]] [[TRM Phenotype]] [[TRM Recirculation]]
> 
>**Experiment** : Transfer of P14 CD8 T cells in mouse, then infection with LCMV Armstrong. Sacrifice 120-150 dpi with intravascular CD8a staining, and quantification of the number of cells.
>**Results** : Number of isolated cells for flow cytometry differed from IHC in FRT. The number of cells obtained with an image-base quantitative IF microscopy technique that was developped was consistent with previous reports, and so differed from cytometry in FRT. In stomach, lung, large intestine and FRT, there were 50-70 time more cells in the image-based quantification than cell isolation, and about 6-27 in other NLT, and about 2 in spleen and LN. Prior to this, it was thought that there was 50-400 less P14 CD8 T cells in NLT compared to SLO, but this analysis showed that it was about 8 times less.
>
>**Other Results** : By comparing which fraction is isolated : cells from blood/marginated pool are more easily isolated, and tissue (unstained by intravascular staining) are underestimated when isolated. Also true for spleen red pulp over white pulp. Also true for epithelium over LP in stomach and SI. CD103 is overrepresented, likely because it is enriched in epithelium.
>
>**Experiment** : Parabiosis for true residency, intravascular staining and quantification
>**Results** : Most NLT unstained cells were resident, with liver having the smallest ratio (~55%). The vast majority of P14 anitgen stimulated cells were TRM and not TEM. Only half of the memory population was in SLO, and blood only had 4% of P14 T cells.
>
>**Other Results** : Thymus and medulla was permissive to memory T cells recirculation. In kidney, even marginated pool was actually TRM.
>
>**Other Results** : By looking at CD69, only 25-75% of cells in pancreas, SG, FRT expressed this marker but almost all cells was TRM. Also in kidney and liver. Marginal TRM cells expressed CD69. Liver in immune parabiont had CD69 in bore vessel of the liver.
>
>**Other Results** : ~20% of P14 T cells were observed in lymphatic vessel in a naive mouse, suggesting tissue egress and TEM in SRT and SG. None of these cells expressed CD69.
>
>**Other Results** : ~30% of CD69- P14 CD8 T cells in parabiont were CD62L+.
>**Experiment** : Transfer of CD62+ or CD62L- OT-I T cells in P14 immune mouse, and challenge with gp33
>**Results** : TCM and TEM were recruited equivalently in tissue following rechallenge. This seems in contradiction with the paradigm of TCM migrating only in SLO.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Memory CD8 T cells protect against intracellular pathogens by scanning host cell surfaces, thus infection detection rates depend on memory cell number and distribution. Population analyses rely on isolation from whole organs and interpretation is predicated on presumptions of near complete cell recovery. Paradigmatically, memory is parsed into central, effector, and resident subsets, ostensibly defined by immunosurveillance patterns, but in practice identified by phenotypic markers. Because isolation methods ultimately inform models of memory T cell differentiation, protection, and vaccine translation, we tested their validity via parabiosis and quantitative immunofluorescence microscopy of a mouse memory CD8 T cell population. We report three major findings: lymphocyte isolation fails to recover most cells and biases against certain subsets, residents greatly outnumber recirculating cells within nonlymphoid tissues, and memory subset homing to inflammation does not conform to previously hypothesized migration patterns. These results indicate that most host cells are surveyed for reinfection by segregated residents rather than by recirculating cells that migrate throughout the blood and body.
