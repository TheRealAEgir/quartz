## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article used VHL-KO T cells to demonstrate the role of hypoxia in tumor TRM. VHL KO cells are HIF1a signaling cells, even in normoxie. KO of VHL resulted in better tumor control, higher CD8 infiltration in non-hypoxic areas. Co-transfer of WT/KO showed that VHL-KO cells had higher inhibitory receptor expression, but higher cytokine secretion and tumor killing capacities in a antigen-independent manner. There was more TRM-like phenotype cells in the VHL-KO conditions, with CD103+ VHL-KO having specifically higher cytokine production/cytotoxicity. Transcriptomic analyses confirmed the previous results. There was limited differences in VHL-KO T cells after anti-PD1 treatment, but VHL-KO T cells had higher tumor control than WT cells unders these conditions. There was higher memory Tcirc independent tumor rechallenge control in VHL-KO. All the observed effect could be dependent on CD103, with most of the observation being lost after intra-vascular anti-CD103 antibody injection.

>**Model** : VHLfl/fl with recombinase under LCK promoter mouse. VHL is the gene that ubiquitinylate HIF1a, leading to it's degradation under normoxie. This was used in P14 T cells and tumor bearing the gp33 LCMV derivec peptide.
>**Results** : VHL KO had reduced tumor growth and better survival, with fewer metastases. There was higher infiltration of CD8+ T cells in KO mouse, but in nonhypoxic areas.
>
>**Experiment** : Co-transfer of WT/KO
>**Results** : There was more KO cells in a kinetic manner, lickely to a survival advantage. KO cells had higher expression of PD1/TIM3/LAG3 but higher secretion of IFNg/TNFa/GZMB upon restimulation and greater ex-vivo tumor-matching killing (granzyme B/Perforine dependent). Co-transfer of antigen bystander OT-I T cells removed the observation, suggesting that it was antigen-dependent. Removal of VHL after the transfer via inductible deletion showed similar results.
>
>**Experiment** : YFP-HIF1a activity reporter mouse.
>**Results** : Similar results than VHL KO, suggesting that HIF1a signaling indeed promote this phenotype.
>
>**Other Results** : KO cells harbored a Trm CD69+CD103+ phenotype with no label upon intra vasculare anti-CD8a injection. CD103+ had higher GZMB expresion and had more tumor cell killing capacities. Inside the CD103+ cell subset, KO cells had higher tumor cell killing capacities, but not inside the CD103- susbet. KO cells had lower TBET/CD62L/Ly6C and higher CD44. YFP-HIF1a reporter+ cells had higher PD1/GITR. Downregulation of CD43/CD27/CXCR3
>
>**Experiment** : Transcriptomic analyses
>**Results** : KO cells had higher effector/costimulatory/coinhibitory gene expression, Trm gene signature and Trm FT (EGR2, RUNX3, PRDM1,  + BCL6, TCF7, EOMES downregulation) and Tex gene signature and LLEC gene signature.
>
>**Experiment** : Transfer of WT or KO T cells and anti-PD1 treatment in tumor bearing mice.
>**Results** : VHL KO + anti-PD1 had the best results, and T cells had higher IFNg/TNFa and had the greater CD69/CD103 expression. There was no changes in T cells number nor granzyme B expression.
>
>**Experiment** : Re-challenge of mouse to test memory responses
>**Results** : More cells and recovered mouse with KO. KO had more Trm phenotypes cells. KO had more IFNg/TNFa co-expressing cells. The memory effect was Trm-dependent, with no effect of the FTY720 treatment.
>
>**Experiment** : Evaluation of CD103 importance by intra vascular CD103 antibody injection.
>**Results** : The selective loss of advantage in KO cells suggested that KO had better tumor control through CD103. There was no effect on WT cells, suggesting no effect on the CD103 on other CD103-expressing cell type. There was a reduction in enhanced granzyme B expression, coproduction of IFNg/TNFa, tumor killing and EOMES/Tbet suppression on the KO cells over the WT cells.
>
>**Experiment** : Transfer of WT/KO CAR-T cells (anti CD19) in CD19-expressing-tumor-bearing mice.
>**Results** : Similar results as before.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Adoptive T cell therapies (ACTs) hold great promise in cancer treatment, but low overall response rates in patients with solid tumors underscore remaining challenges in realizing the potential of this cellular immunotherapy approach. Promoting CD8+ T cell adaptation to tissue residency represents an underutilized but promising strategy to improve tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) function. Here, we report that deletion of the HIF negative regulator von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) in CD8+ T cells induced HIF-1α/HIF-2α–dependent differentiation of tissue-resident memory–like (Trm-like) TILs in mouse models of malignancy. VHL-deficient TILs accumulated in tumors and exhibited a core Trm signature despite an exhaustion-associated phenotype, which led to retained polyfunctionality and response to αPD-1 immunotherapy, resulting in tumor eradication and protective tissue-resident memory. VHL deficiency similarly facilitated enhanced accumulation of chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells with a Trm-like phenotype in tumors. Thus, HIF activity in CD8+ TILs promotes accumulation and antitumor activity, providing a new strategy to enhance the efficacy of ACTs.,
