## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article used a mouse model and described that TNBC had a CD69+CD103+ TRM population associated with better outcome, in contrast to a CD69+CD103- TEX population. These population were dependent on TGFb, but not on IL15/TBET/EOMES. scRNAseq showed that these population were distinct. Treatment of mice with anti-PD1/anti-CTLA4 showed increased of both population, with an increase in IFNg and TNFa production; however, the TRM population had a higher increase in tumor lysis assay in these settings, which was dependent on IFNg and TNFa. These population confered increased protection in a re-challenge experiment independently of circulating T cell, were they slowly changed there phenotype to a CD69+CD103+ DP only. Isolation of a TRM specific signature in this model and applying it on several human cohort showed that a higher score was associated with better outcome. > 
>**Model** : AT3-OVA cell line injection in the fourth mammary fat pad of a mouse, and TILs isolation
>**Results** : CD69+CD103+CD44hiCD8+ TRM mostly found in tumor tissue with peak at 6 week. The CD69+CD103- SP population was the one with the higher OVA-tetramer staining. Cells resembled TRM cells at the transcriptomic level, both by up/downregulation of known gene and by comparing gene signature with TRM from skin/gut/liver of infected mice and human TNBC. The CD69+CD103- SP population was more exhausted than the other population.
>
>**Experiment** : Co-transfer of WT or KO OT-I cell for TGFbRII, Tbet, Eomes and IL15
>**Results** : TGFb is required because the proportion of KO cell was reduced. However, there was no requirement for either Tbet, Eomes and IL15, meaning that IL15 is not required.
>
>**Experiment** : scRNAseq in this tumor model
>**Results** : There was a cluster with elevated expression of CD103 and one with elevated expression of TOX, and those resembled signature of the DP and SP population, respectively. There was little TCR overlap, meaning that the populations were distinct.
>
>**Experiment** : Same tumor model, but with anti-PD1 or anti-PD1/anti-CTLA4 treatment.
>**Results** : There was improved survival in mice, and expansion of CD8 T cells over CD4. There was an increase in both DP and SP populations, and both secreted higher IFNg and TNFa, even inside the OVA-specific population. The DP had higher tumor cell lysis (with chromium-labeled tumor cells) especially after 24h and in the anti-PD1+anti-CTLA4 condition. This was dependent on IFNg and TNFa secretion, as blocking these with specific antibody inhibited the tumor cell killing.
>**Other Results** : There was an increased of TCR clonotypes after dual anti-PD1/anti-CTLA4 treatment.
>
>**Experiment** : After the experiment, some cell survived, and T cell persistence was assessed in a re-challenge with or without FTY720
>**Results** : There was an increased amount of DP TRM in the FTY720 treated group, which expressed IFNg and TNFa. This group also had higher secondary tumor protection.
>
>**Experiment** : Isolation of a CD69+CD103+ DP vs CD69+CD103- SP signature and testing in human cohort
>**Results** : The signature had minimal overlap with existing signature in human TNBC and mouse TRM, yet was increased in a human melanoma TRM vs TEX signature. Higher scoring of the signature was associated with better outcome in the METABRIC cohort (breast cancer with no immunotherapy), as well as in a ISPY clinical trial with immunotherapy, and also in another one of TNBC with anti PDL1, and also in a anti-PD1/anti-CTLA-4 melanoma cohort.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes with a tissue-resident memory T (TRM) cell phenotype are associated with favorable prognosis in patients with triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). However, the relative contribution of CD8+ TRM cells to anti-tumor immunity and immune checkpoint blockade efficacy in breast cancer remains unknown. Here, we show that intratumoral CD8+ T cells in murine mammary tumors transcriptionally resemble those from TNBC patients. Phenotypic and transcriptional studies established two intratumoral sub-populations: one more enriched in markers of terminal exhaustion (TEX-like) and the other with a bona fide resident phenotype (TRM-like). Treatment with anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 therapy resulted in expansion of these intratumoral populations, with the TRM-like subset displaying significantly enhanced cytotoxic capacity. TRM-like CD8+ T cells could also provide local immune protection against tumor rechallenge and a TRM gene signature extracted from tumor-free tissue was significantly associated with improved clinical outcomes in TNBC patients treated with checkpoint inhibitors.
