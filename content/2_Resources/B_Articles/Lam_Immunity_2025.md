> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article studied age-related T cell changes in healthy human. [[TRM Phenotype]] [[TRM Heterogeneity]] [[TRM Epigenetic]] [[TRM Caracteristics]]
> There is a hypomethylation of DNA in T cells with age. CD244 est un marqueur de sénéscence.
>
>**Experiment** : Multi-omic analyses of healthy human organs, including 14C dating and bisulfite sequencing.
>**Results** : There is less cells in mLN with age, but not in other tissues and increase of fibrosis in spleen. Despite these, there is no changes in CD3+ T cell density in OL-II and lung/intestine. There is more CD4 over CD8 with age in lng/mLN. There is also loss of naive CD8 T cell for TEM/TRM/TEMRA depending on the tissue, and CD4 are more stable. 14C dating showed lifespan of 1-2 years for T cells, but was imprecise. Ki67 dating showed 1-2 years for every tissue except spleen which last for 3-9 years with no changes with age. CD4 have lower lifespan than CD8. TEM divide more than TRM. TRM phenotype (CD103, CD101, CXCR6, CD49a, PD1) does not vary with age except for a decrease of CD103 expression by TRM in LN.
>**Other Results** : Increase of senescent cells CD57/KLRG1/CD244 with age in blood/spleen/lung. TRM cells did not express CD57 and KLRG1, with intermediate expression CD69+CD103- vs CD69+CD103+.
>**Other Results** : There is hypomethlation of DNA with age in TEM and TRM, which do not vary between tissues. This is also true for solo WGCW site (CpG entoured by either adenosine of thymine, and located at more than 35bp than the closer CpG site, which was shown to be the most affected by age/proliferation driven hypomethylation). There was hypermethylation in SOX4/WNT1/CCR10/GATA3/IL2RG/CD103 in TRM.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Memory T cells are maintained in tissues as circulating effector-memory (TEM) and tissue-resident (TRM) populations for protective immunity, though the role of site and subset in memory persistence remains undefined. Here, we investigated age-associated dynamics of human T cells in lymphoid organs, mucosal sites, and blood over 10 decades of life using retrospective radiocarbon (14C) birth dating, along with cellular, transcriptome, and epigenetic profiling. Memory T cells across peripheral sites exhibited continuous turnover with mean lifespans of 1–2 years, while the spleen contained longer-lived T cells. Over age, TEM cells expressed senescent markers and a GZMK transcriptional signature, while TRM cells maintained site-specific resident phenotypes without exhibiting features of senescence. Both TEM and TRM cells showed age-associated DNA hypomethylation, though TRM cells exhibited more epigenetically regulated genes. Together, our findings reveal asynchronous aging of human memory T cells by subset and site, as well as persistence of TRM cells without immunosenescence.
