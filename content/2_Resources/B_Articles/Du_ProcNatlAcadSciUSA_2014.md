## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article used EGR2 CKO T cells mouse to demonstrate a role for EGR2 in T cells activation. EGR2 CKO T cells were less proliferative and secreted less cytokine upon CD3/CD28 activation, partially rescued by IL2. Transcriptomic analyses and ChIPseq revealed decrease in T cell proliferation and transcription factors, including NOTCH1 and TBET for which EGR2 can directly bind to (also, it bind to IFNg and TNFa). EGR2 CKO mouse had poorer response to influenza infection, less cytokine production but higher lung T cells infiltration. These cell were enriched in MPEC over SLEC, but didn't had expression of CD69 and CD103, suggesting a role for EGR2 in [[TRM Differenciation]].
> 
>**Experiment** : conditionnal deletion of EGR2 in T cells and EGR1 KO mouse
>**Results** : There was some increase in DP over SP thymocytes with both. There was fewer spleen T cells in EGR2 KO, with some increase in Treg (proportion only). In the spleen, there was an increase in effector CD4 T cells but not CD8 (CD44hi/CD62Llo).
>There was some decrease in proliferation following CD3/CD28, restored by IL2. There was less secretion of IL2 in EGR2 KO only. EGR2 KO only had less secretion of IFNg/IL4/IL9/IL17a in CD4 T cells and IFNg in CD8 T cells. Addition of IL2 rescued IFNg and partially rescued IL4 prodution.
>
>**Experiment** : Transcriptomic analyses
>**Results** : EGR2 CKO T cells had downregulation of gene involved in T cell proliferation and TF including NOTCH1/STAT5A/TBET/GATA3. ChIPseq confirmed that EGR2 bind to TBET and NOTCH1.
>
>**Experiment** : Infection with severe influenza in WT/KO mice
>**Results** : EGR2 CKO mice had worse pathological response and reduced expression of IL1B/IL6/CCL5 and TH1/TH17 cytokines CXCL10/IL17a. There was more CD4 and CD8 T cells in the lung. However, there was similar number of specific T cells for the immunodominant influenza peptide in EGR2 CKO in the MLN, but more in the lung (not in EGR1 KO). There was more MPEC at the response peak in EGR2 CKO and there was more EGR2 expression in isolated MPEC. However, lung TRM had lower CD69/CD103 expression in EGR2 KO. ChIPseq of EGR2 shower that EGR2 could bind to IFNg and TNF genes. There was an increase of GZMB/PRF1 expression in EGR2 CKO, but the genes were not in the ChIPseq data.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Early growth response 2 (EGR2) is a transcription factor that can negatively regulate T-cell activation. We unexpectedly found that EGR2 promotes peripheral naïve T-cell proliferation and differentiation, with less T-cell receptor-induced IL-2 production in Egr2-deficient naïve T cells and diminished cytokine production in T-helper differentiated cells. Moreover, EGR2 was required for T-cell responses to influenza, with delayed viral clearance and more severe pathology in lungs of Egr2 conditional knockout mice, as well as decreased effector cytokine production from T cells. Thus, EGR2 can act as a positive regulator essential for a normal T-cell response to viral infection, a finding with potential clinical implications., Early growth response 2 (EGR2) transcription factor negatively regulates T-cell activation, in contrast to the positive regulation of this process by EGR1. Here, we unexpectedly found that EGR2 promotes peripheral naïve T-cell differentiation, with delayed T-cell receptor-induced proliferation in naïve T cells from Egr2 conditional knockout (CKO) mice and decreased production of IFN-γ, IL-4, IL-9, and IL-17A in cells subjected to T-helper differentiation. Moreover, genes that promote T-cell activation, including Tbx21 and Notch1, had decreased expression in Egr2 CKO T cells and are direct EGR2 target genes. Following influenza infection, Egr2 CKO mice had delayed viral clearance, more weight loss, and more severe pathological changes in the lung than did WT and Egr1 KO mice, with decreased production of effector cytokines, increased infiltration of antigen-specific memory-precursor CD8+ T cells, and lower numbers of lung-resident memory CD8+ T cells. Thus, unexpectedly, EGR2 can function as a positive regulator that is essential for naïve T-cell differentiation and in vivo T-cell responses to a viral infection.
