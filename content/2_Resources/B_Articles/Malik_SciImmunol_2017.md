## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : Study of TRM formed in vitiligo after melanoma elimination, that can protect against rechallenge and have some spreading. Loss of FucT-VII (CLA) lead to reduction in TRM skin access and lymphoid memory formation, and loss of CD103 only impair skin. [[TRM Cancer]]
> [[TRM Therapies]] [[TRM Caracteristics]] [[TRM Precursors]]
> 
>**Experiment** : model of vitiligo induced by tumor inoculation, Treg depletion and tumor excision. With pmel.
>**Results** : Enrichment of CD8 T pmel cells in vitiligo with a TRM phenotype with low PD1/LAG3 but cytokine secretion upon restimulation.
>
>**Experiment** : Graft of vitiligo skin onto RAG-/- mouse
>**Results** : TRM did not recirculated aside from a small pmel- CD103- CLA- population.
>
>**Experiment** : CLA KO mouse or CD103
>**Results** : less vitiligo when CLA KO, no effect of CD103
>**Experiment** : CLA WT/KO co-transfer or CD103
>**Results** : less KO cells in vitiligo but not in tdLN (and also for CD103)
>
>**Experiment** : CD8 KO mouse reconstitued with CD103 ko or WT T cells re-challenged with B16
>**Results** : Only WT were protected from re-challenge, suggesting a role for CD103. This was unnafected by FTY720, suggesting a TRM only effect.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Tissue-resident memory T cells (TRM cells) have been widely characterized in infectious disease settings; however, their role in mediating immunity to cancer remains unknown. Here we report that skin-resident memory T cell responses to melanoma are generated naturally as a result of autoimmune vitiligo. Melanoma antigen-specific TRM cells resided predominantly in melanocyte-depleted hair follicles and were maintained without recirculation or replenishment from the lymphoid compartment. These cells expressed CD103, CD69, and CLA, but lacked PD-1 or LAG-3, and were capable of making IFN-γ. CD103 expression on CD8 T cells was required for establishment of TRM cells in skin, but was dispensable for vitiligo development. Importantly, CD103+ CD8 TRM cells were critical for protection against melanoma re-challenge. This work establishes that CD103-dependent TRM cells play a key role in perpetuating anti-tumor immunity.
