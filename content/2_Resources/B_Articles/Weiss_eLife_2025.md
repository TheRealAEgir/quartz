> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article used a mouse model of VV-OVA infection in a flank and DNFB in another flank, resulting in TRM in presence/absence of antigen. This showed no difference in the cell number/specificity/expression of CD103. Transcriptome analysis showed that the TRM in the antigen flank seemed more differenciated. Re-activation with peptide injection resulted in specific expansion of the antigen flank TRM, which was due to cell proliferation. TGFbRIII KO showed that there was a reduction in the DNFB flank, meaning that TGFbRIII is required in TGFb limiting environment. > 
>**Model** : Infection of mouse with VV in the left flank, and DNFB in the right flank, which both recruit Teff in the skin that differenciate into TRM
>**Results** : There was no difference in cell number, CD103 expression and VV immunodominant antigen, suggesting that polyclonal TRM are not affected by the presence or absence of antigen in the skin once recruited.
>
>**Experiment** : Use of scRNAseq data from the ImmgenT consortium, which pulled together data from mouse experiment from different teams.
>**Results** : There was a cluster that was enriched in the condition were the antigen was present. This cluster was enriched in TRM signature. By comparing with the other data, the transcriptome of this cluster seemed to be more differentiated that those of bystander TRM. It was expressing DUSP1/NR4A1/JUNB/FOS.
>
>**Experiment** : Same mouse model as before, but with OT-I and VV-OVA
>**Results** : Re-activation of the cell in both flank lead to higher expansion in the VV-OVA flank compared to the DNFB flank. This was not due to Tcirc recruitement (FTY720/anti-Thy1.1). Same when tested in long-term fashion. This was due to proliferation (BrdU) and not because of apoptosis (Annexin V). Activation with a low-affinity peptide lead to reduced expansion, meaning that the strength of TCR signaling was important.
>
>**Model** : Mouse with specific inducible deletion of TGRBRIII in CD8+ T cells. This model was able to generate ~50% of TGFBRIII KO OT-I cells, which were visible by a raporter gene that was expressed in KO cell only. + same model as before
>**Results** : There was a reduction in the number of TRM in the DNFB flank, but not in the VV-OVA flank. These cells were depleted when using an inhibitor that block the keratinocyte's TGFb activation. This was not the case in steady state epidermis.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > CD8+ tissue-resident memory T cells (TRM) develop from effectors that seed peripheral tissues where they persist providing defense against subsequent challenges. TRM persistence requires autocrine TGFβ transactivated by integrins expressed on keratinocytes. TRM precursors that encounter antigen in the epidermis during development outcompete bystander TRM for TGFβ resulting in enhanced persistence. ScRNA-seq analysis of epidermal TRM revealed that local antigen experience in the skin resulted in an enhanced differentiation signature in comparison with bystanders. Upon recall, TRM displayed greater proliferation dictated by affinity of antigen experienced during epidermal development. Finally, local antigen experienced TRM differentially expressed TGFβRIII, which increases avidity of the TGFβRI/II receptor complex for TGFβ. Selective ablation of Tgfbr3 reduced local antigen experienced TRM capacity to persist, rendering them phenotypically like bystander TRM. Thus, antigen-driven TCR signaling in the epidermis during TRM differentiation results in a lower TGFβ requirement for persistence and increased proliferative capacity that together enhance epidermal TRM fitness., We are constantly exposed to a wide array of pathogens in our environment. This would be detrimental to our survival if it were not for the immune system’s ability to adapt. Each time the body encounters pathogens, it develops an immunological memory that allows it to mount a quick defense response. Recent research has shown that immunological memory is not confined to immune organs like lymph nodes and spleen. Instead, a large proportion is maintained in peripheral tissues at sites of prior infection., These local cells, known as tissue-resident memory T cells (TRM), provide the first line of defense against repeated infections. TRM develop from circulating precursors of memory T cells after pathogen exposure and then permanently reside in these tissues. The ability to mount pathogen-specific responses is a hallmark of immunological memory. Therefore, knowing how prior antigen exposure shapes TRM development is critical for understanding peripheral immunity. However, the signals that drive T cells to become TRM remain incompletely understood., Using an acute viral infection model in mice, Weiss et al. investigated how local infection affects the differentiation and function of TRM in the skin. TRM cells were generated through skin infection with a poxvirus strain, and immune responses were measured using immunofluorescence, flow cytometry, and approaches that distinguish circulating from resident T cells., The results showed that T cells must encounter pathogen-derived antigens directly in the skin, in addition to in the lymph nodes, for the effective development of TRM, which are capable of mounting stronger recall responses. Their long-term survival in the skin depended on signaling through the transforming growth factor-β (TGFβ) pathway, which is activated by skin cells and enhanced in T cells that encountered pathogens within the epidermis., TRM play important roles in cancer surveillance, pathogen clearance and vaccine response, but they can also contribute to disease when dysregulated, as seen in conditions such as psoriasis, vitiligo and graft-versus-host disease. Understanding the factors that promote TRM fitness may enable strategies for making these cells more effective in fighting infections. Further, TGFß represents a possible therapeutic target to selectively modulate TRM activity when these cells become harmful.
