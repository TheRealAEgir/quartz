## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article used a vaccine containing a peptide with or without 4-1BBL and made intra nasal immunisation in mouse. The inclusion of 4-1BBL resulted in greater expansion of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells and better infection control. The cells in blood/SLO had a phenotype which was closer to Tcm cells, and those in the lung were CD69+CD103-+ Trm. When mixing bone marrow from a 4-1BB KO/WT mice and infection, the majority of antigen specific CD8 T cells were from the WT mice. CD8+ T cells in the 4-1BBL group showed higher expression of CD127, but IL7 dependency couldn't be proved. Finally, FTY720 injection showed that 4-1BBL has an effect on the recruitment of Tcirc into NLT for Trm differentiation rather that Trm proliferation. [[TRM Therapies]] [[TRM Migration]] [[TRM Differenciation]]
> 
>**Experiment** : Primary infection of mice, then boosting with an empty adenovirus, an adenovirus containing the influenza peptide, or an adenovirus containing the influenza peptide and 4-1BBL, and T cells were followed in the blood and spleen
>**Results** : Inclusion of 4-1BBL made a greater expansion of CD8 T cells, which were long lived.
>**Experiment** : After immunization, challenge with lethal dose of influenza. Inluenza was chosen to have a different HA/NA protein to exclude potential antibody-mediated response.
>**Results** : Mice who had received the 4-1BBL containing vaccine had less weight loss and better survival. 
>
>**Other Results** : Mice who had received 4-1BBL containing vaccin had delayed CD62L acquisition in blood/SLO without difference in CD127, suggesting a bias from Tcm toward Tem cells. There also was an increase in Tbet and a decrease in EOMES and TCF1. These were also more polyfunctionnal (IFNg/TNFa/IL2/CD107a)
>
>**Experiment** : Pre-euthanasia anti-CD8 antibody injection and lung TRM studies
>**Results** : There was an increase in antigen-specific TRM in the lung when 4-1BBL. TRM were CD69+-CD103+-, with low Tbet/EOMES/CD127/TCF1 and high CD49a
>
>**Experiment** : Reconstitution of mouse immune system with 1:1 bone marrow cells from 4-1BB WT and KO, and similar vaccination
>**Results** : The majority of antigen-specific CD8 T cells both a response peak and 50 days post boost were from the 4-1BB WT mouse.
>
>**Other Results** : Antigen-specific CD8+ from the 4-1BBL containing group had higher CD127, with no difference in CD25/CD122.
>**Experiment** : Combination of the previous experiment with an anti-CD127 blocking antibody.
>**Results** : There wasn't a difference in the groups at 0 and 2 days post-boost, but this maybe was the result of the blocking antibody not reaching the NLT.
>
>**Experiment** : Systemic i.p. vs local i.n. immunisation
>**Results** : Systemic immunisation made greater number of memory cells in the spleen, but not efficient in the lung. 4-1BBL did not improve this result.
>
>**Experiment** : Immunisation plus PBS/FTY720 injection, preventing the egress and recruitment of Tcirc in the tissue.
>**Results** : FTY720 decreased the number of Trm in the lung in the 4-1BBL condition to a level comparable to the vaccine without 4-1BBL, which wasn't affected by the treatment. FTY720 did not reduced the proportion of antigen-specific T cells, suggesting that the previous result is due to 4-1BBL having an action on Tcirc recruitment rather that Trm proliferation.
>
>**Experiment** : Mixing of 4-1BB WT/KO and injection in a mice, and infection
>**Results** : Antigen-specific lung CD8+ T cells were almost entirely from the WT part, but not in the dLN and spleen.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > The induction of long-lived heterotypic T-cell protection against influenza virus remains elusive, despite the conservation of T-cell epitopes. T-cell protection against influenza is critically dependent on lung-resident memory T cells (Trm). Here we show that intranasal administration of 4-1BBL along with influenza nucleoprotein in a replication-defective adenovirus vector to influenza pre-immune mice induces a remarkably stable circulating effector memory CD8 T-cell population characterized by higher IL-7Rα expression than control-boosted T cells, as well as a substantial lung parenchymal CD69+ CD8 Trm population, including both CD103+ and CD103- cells. These T-cell responses persist to greater than 200 days post-boost and protect against lethal influenza challenge in aged (year old) mice. The expansion of the nucleoprotein-specific CD8 Trm population during boosting involves recruitment of circulating antigen-specific cells and is critically dependent on local rather than systemic administration of 4-1BBL as well as on 4-1BB on the CD8 T cells. Moreover, during primary influenza infection of mixed bone marrow chimeras, 4-1BB-deficient T cells fail to contribute to the lung-resident Trm population. These findings establish both endogenous and supraphysiological 4-1BBL as a critical regulator of lung-resident memory CD8 T cells during influenza infection.
