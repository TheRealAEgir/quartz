## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article describe the heterogeneity between skin and liver TRM. In comparaison : skin TRM expressed CD103, had lower cytokine secretion and lower homeostatic proliferation, but lived for a longer period. Only liver TRM was able to repopulate the parabiont mouse. Co-transfer or TBET/EOMES/TGFbRII WT/KO T cells showed that only skin TRM depended on TGFb. Transfer of TRM in mouse showed that liver TRM had higher repopulation capacitites, and that ex liver TRM can adopt a skin like phenotype once entering the skin. SG seemed composed of a mixed TRM population that was composed of TRM that resembled skin TRM and another that resembled liver TRM in every aspect. [[TRM Phenotype]] [[TRM Persistance]] [[TRM Localization]] [[TRM Recirculation]] [[TRM Heterogeneity]] [[TRM Differenciation]]
> 
>**Model** : HSV infection to generate TRM in the skin, and LCMV Armstrong to generate TRM in other tissues, with injection of CD45.1 gBT-I or P14 T cells.
>**Results** : Skin TRM were CD69+CD103+, liver TRM were CD69+CD103-, and SG TRM were mixed. These cells has different protein expression pattern. Similar profile was found in TRM formed from OT-I and DNFB injection.
>
>**Experiment** : OT-I and DNFB infection, then peptide activation
>**Results** : Skin TRM produced lower IFNg, TNFa and IL-2, showed lower homeostatic proliferation. Most liver TRM were lost after 60 days (also with the infection model)
>
>**Experiment** : Parabiosis in the same setting as precedent
>**Results** : Skin TRM did not went into the parabiont, but some liver TRM did.
>
>**Experiment** : Co-transfer of WT and TBET/EOMES KO OT-I cells in mice infection with virus-OVA.
>**Results** : Tbet KO reduced the number of TRM whereas EOMES KO did not, in both skin and liver. Co-transfer of effector WT/KO TGFBRII cells reduced the number of TRM in the skin but not in the liver. These results argue that both TRM subtypes are dependent on IL15, but only skin TRM are dependent on TGFb. Liver TRM were only slightly diminished when OE Tbet or EOMES, contrary to skin TRM that completely disappear.
>**Other Results** : With bulk RNAseq of WT/TGFbRII KO cells from skin and liver; great changes in skin TRM, including upregulation of CD103, CDH1, QPCT, 2B4, RUNX3, NR4A1, PD1 and downregulation of S1PR1, S1PR5, EOMES in WT cells. Skin TRM resembled liver TRM in absence of TGFbRII.
>**Other Results** : TGFb KO skin TRM had lower expression of PD1, ICOS, TIM3, TIGIT. Treatement of skin with TGFb only reduced the cytokine secretion of the WT cells, whereas both WT and KO liver TRM were unaffected by TGFb treatement.
>
>**Experiment** : TGFb and TGFbRII expression is high on both skin and liver, but liver TRM do not seems to have TGFb signaling. Forced TGFb signaling was achieved by using TGFbRI constituve 
>**Results** : Liver TRM was depleted rather than phenocopying skin TRM.
>
>**Experiment** : Transfer of skin and liver TRM in mouse
>**Results** : Skin TRM showed lower repopulation of the new host mouse than liver TRM, although both were lower than Tcirc.
>**Other Results** : Liver TRM that were transfered in the new host mouse adopted a skin TRM-like phenotype in the skin. The depletion of TGFbRII reduced their CD103 expression and persistence in the skin.
>
>**Model** : anti-Gr-1 to deplete LyC6+ T circ, and anti-CXCR3 to deplete TRM, generate a mouse which only had ex-TRM. All of this in a parabiosis model.
>**Results** : exTRM repopulated the parabiont after OVA activation.
>
>**Experiment** : TRM from SG
>**Results** : TRM in the epithelium are CD103 and resemble skin TRM, including the dependence on TGFb and transcriptome, while TRM in the peripheral structure resembled liver TRM. Same observation concerning the repopulation potential.
>
>**Discussion** : liver TRM may overide the TGFb dependence by not expressing CD103 and relying on LFA1 instead.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Tissue-resident memory T (TRM) cells are non-recirculating cells that exist throughout the body. Although TRM cells in various organs rely on common transcriptional networks to establish tissue residency, location-specific factors adapt these cells to their tissue of lodgment. Here we analyze TRM cell heterogeneity between organs and find that the different environments in which these cells differentiate dictate TRM cell function, durability and malleability. We find that unequal responsiveness to TGFβ is a major driver of this diversity. Notably, dampened TGFβ signaling results in CD103− TRM cells with increased proliferative potential, enhanced function and reduced longevity compared with their TGFβ-responsive CD103+ TRM counterparts. Furthermore, whereas CD103− TRM cells readily modified their phenotype upon relocation, CD103+ TRM cells were comparatively resistant to transdifferentiation. Thus, despite common requirements for TRM cell development, tissue adaptation of these cells confers discrete functional properties such that TRM cells exist along a spectrum of differentiation potential that is governed by their local tissue microenvironment.
