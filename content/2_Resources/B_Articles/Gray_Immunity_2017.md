## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article measured H3K27ac/me3 in SLEC vs MPEC, and found that SLEC had repression of memory associated genes while MPEC has no repression. By conditionally removing EZH2 from gzmb effector cells, the authors showed that (1) EZH2 was responsible for the majority of the H3K27me3 marks (2) T cells lost expansion through increased apoptosis and fail to control infection (3) KLRG1+ effector cells had a phenotype that was closer to memory cells. The loss of EZH2 impaired naive to effector and memory to effector transition. EZH2 was dispensable to initial transcriptional repression, but stabilised it. Finally, FOXO1 expression in memory cells could be responsible for limiting EZH2 activity. [[1.1 Différenciation des lymphocytes T en lymphocyte T effecteur ou mémoire]] 
> 
>**Experiment** : Transfer of P14 in naice mice and infection with LCMV Armstrong, isolation of KLRG1+CD127- (TE) and KLRG1-/CD127+ (MP) for H3K27ac/H3K27me3 ChIP-seq.
>**Results** : The major differences were a repression of memory-associated genes in TE : ID3, TCF7, BACH2, and Trm genes : SKI1, SKIL, CDH1. KLRG1, TBET and BLIMP1 were not repressed. In contrast, MP showed open chromatine for most genes, including TE genes. This suggested that TE loose memory potential through epigenetic silencing.
>
>**Experiment** : EZH2 floxed conditionnal KO under the CD4 promoter-cre expression -> All thymocyte permanently loose EZH2 expression.
>**Results** : Removing EZH2 lead to reduction of almost all H3K27me3 in T cells.
>**Experiment** : Conditionnal EZH2 KO with granzyme B promoter -> only after activation/infection
>**Results** : T cells lost their expansion potential and failed viral control. The KO cells had similar division rate to WT, suggesting the observation was due to higher apoptosis. The KO cells were also polyfunctional with IFNg/IL2 and a little less TNFa. There was initialy comparable amount of KLRG1+ effector cells, but it was reduced by 8 dpi were they expressed less KLRG1/CD127/TBET and more CD27/CD62L/TCF1/FOXO1/EOMES
>
>**Model** : Conditionnal inductible EZH2 KO; estrogen-receptor (ER)-Cre under the promoter of granzyme B, and only tamoxifen treatement induce the nulcear localization or the ER-Cre. The cells also had a ROAS26-flow-STOP-flox-YFP, to report Cre activity by permanent YFP expression. + LCMV Amrstrong infection and tamoxifen injection between 8-23 dpi.
>**Results** : The loss of EZH2 post primary response did not impaired memory formation. This suggested a role for EZH2 in naive to effector, but not effector to memory transition. Co transfer of memory EZH2 WT/KO in mice showed that EZH2 KO fail to mount secondary effector cells as well.
>
>**Other Results** : EZH2 KO showed equivalent reduction of TCF1/FOXO1/BACH2/ID3 mRNA expression to WT at 4.5 dpi, indicating that H3K27me3 was not required for initial repression. Indeed, H3K27me3 increased in TE at 10 dpi, suggesting a role in stabilising repression.
>
>**Experiment** : FOXO1 KO/STAT4 KO/TBET OE and ChIP-qPCR of TCF1/BACH2 anti-H3K27me3
>**Results** : Only FOXO1 KO had an effect on the H3K27me3 in TCF1 and BACH2 promoter of KLRG1hiCD127low T cells. FOXO1 expression was higher in MP and bound to memory genes, but not in the H3K27me3 regions. The authors suggested that higher FOXO1 expression in MP lead to decreased EZH2 activity in the FOXO1 binding sites, thus EZH2 could only repress memory genes in low-FOXO1 TE cells.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Understanding immunological memory formation depends on elucidating how multipotent memory precursor (MP) cells maintain developmental plasticity and longevity to provide long-term immunity while other effector cells develop into terminally differentiated effector (TE) cells with limited survival. Profiling active (H3K27ac) and repressed (H3K27me3) chromatin in naive, MP, and TE CD8+ T cells during viral infection revealed increased H3K27me3 deposition at numerous pro-memory and pro-survival genes in TE relative to MP cells, indicative of fate restriction, but permissive chromatin at both pro-memory and pro-effector genes in MP cells, indicative of multipotency. Polycomb repressive complex 2 deficiency impaired clonal expansion and TE cell differentiation, but minimally impacted CD8+ memory T cell maturation. Abundant H3K27me3 deposition at pro-memory genes occurred late during TE cell development, probably from diminished transcription factor FOXO1 expression. These results outline a temporal model for loss of memory cell potential through selective epigenetic silencing of pro-memory genes in effector T cells.
