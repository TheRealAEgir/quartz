## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article discuss the fact that TEX has been treated as if they were TRM in cancer in the latest research. By using CITEseq/cycling IF, they showed that TRM and TEX are distinct are propose a pan-cancer TRM and TEX signature as well as some surface protein markers, although these may be different depending on the context. Only the TEX population was associated with response to ICB. The two population showed limited TCR overlap, and the TEX was tumor antigen-specific while TRM was virus antigen-specific. TRM was mostly found in healthy tissue, while TEX was found in tumor tissue. These population had common precursors, has shown with SPLINTR barcoding. The main mecanism responsible for the formation of one over the other population was the TCR antigen signaling, wich was higher in TEX. TRM could differenciate into TEX after antigen stimulation, while TEX could differenciate (or die) in TRM in absence of antigen.

> 
>**Results** : This paper started with the observation that TRM and TEX share a lot of similarities including the resident program, and that TRM signatures were labelling TEX as well.
>
>**Experiment** : CITE-RNA-TCR-seq of CD3+ from breast cancer and normal breast tissue.
>**Results** : This resulted in the identification of two TRM cluster that were predominantly found in the healthy tissue and three distinct cluster of TEX that were predominantly found in the tumor, but shared some similarities (CD69, CD103, HOBIT). TEX expressed CD38/CD39/PD1/CTLA4/TIGIT/TIM3.
>**Other Results** : By comparing the two populations, the following markers were described : CD74, CST7, LYSTR, GZMA, EOMES, GZMK, TOX, CXCL13 for TEX and IL7R, CD55, PTGER2, SMIM3, GNLY, MYBL1, TOB1, S100A4, KLRC1, STAT4 for the TRM. These can be used in cyclic immunofluorescence (Cycif) and flow cytometry.
>
>**Experiment** : CycIF analysis of breast cancer patients
>**Results** : Approximation of TRM with PD1lowGNLYhi and TEX with PD1hiGNLYlow. TEX expressed CD39, TIM3 and LAG3, while TRM expressed CD94, CD7 and NKG2A
>
>**Experiment** : *Ex vivo* stimulation of TRM and TEX
>**Results** : TRM expressed more IFNg, TNFa, IL2, GNLY while TEX expressed more GMZA and GZMK
>
>**Other Results** : TRM was associated with overall survival but with poorer response to ICB, and inversely with TEX.
>
>**Experiment** : sc CITEseq of CRC-LM.
>**Results** : Similar results compared to BC. Signatures seemed to be able to be used for the two cohort interchangeably. This was not the case for the surface specific marker CD96, CD101, CD161, CD73. There was still a pan-cancer TRM and TEX signature that was developed.
>
>**Experiment** : Analysis of the TCR repertoire between the two populations.
>**Results** : The two population had different TCR repertoire. The use of TCRdist predicted that they have distinct epitope specificities. By using VDJb, the TRM had more virus specific clones, whereas the TEX had tumor antigen specific.
>
>**Experiment** : Mouse BC model with ATF3-OVA and OT-I cells and high-dimensional flow cytometry
>**Results** : There was clusters of resident OT-I TIL expression CD69 and PD1 and differing in CD103, TCF1, TOX and TIM3 expression. The presence of TRM expressing CD103 was inversely correlated with the amount of tumor cell and GFP-OVA. The removing of TCR lead to increase in CD103 expression (which the TEX did not expressed), suggesting that antigen is important in TEX formation. OT-3 (OT-I, but with lower antigen affinity) had lower CD103 expression too. Co-transfer of OT-I and gBT-I cells showed that bystander gBT-I aquired a TRM phenotype and lower amount of TEX related protein. Signature from bystander T cells correlated with human TEX, while signature from tumor antigen specific correlated with human TRM.
>
>**Experiment** : SPLINTR barcoding of naive OT-I cell and transfer into a AT3-OVA mouse.
>**Results** : In cell with the same antigen specificity; tumor-derived resembled each other in comparison to spleen, meaning that TRM could have common progenitors with other intratumoral populations
>
>**Experiment** : Since there may exist tumor antigen specific TRM cells in this model, this may be due to antigen loos or spatial segregation. In this context, there may be a TRM to TEX differenciation if cell are re-activated with antigen; reinjection of tumor T cell population in another tumor bearing mouse
>**Results** : All population more closely resembled TEX after re-transfer in the tumor.
>**Experiment** : Reinjection of TRM and TEX into tumor with or withou antigen
>**Results** : TRM gain a TEX phenotype in the tumor with antigen, whereas TEX gained a more TRM phenotype in the tumor without antigen, meaning that both population could differenciate into the other population.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > CD8+ T cells are an important weapon in the therapeutic armamentarium against cancer. While CD8+CD103+ T cells with a tissue-resident memory T (TRM) cell phenotype are associated with favorable prognoses, the tumor microenvironment also contains dysfunctional exhausted T (TEX) cells that exhibit a variety of TRM-like features. Here we deconvolute TRM and TEX cells across human cancers, ascribing markers and gene signatures that distinguish these populations and enable their functional distinction. Although TRM cells have superior functionality and are associated with long-term survival post-tumor resection, they are not associated with responsiveness to immune checkpoint blockade. Tumor-associated TEX and TRM cells are clonally distinct, with the latter comprising tumor-independent bystanders and tumor-specific cells segregated from cognate antigen. Intratumoral TRM cells can be forced toward an exhausted fate when chronic antigen stimulation occurs, indicating that the presence or absence of continuous antigen exposure within the microenvironment is the key distinction between tumor-associated TEX and TRM populations. These results highlight unique functions for TRM and TEX cells in tumor control, underscoring the need for distinct strategies to harness these populations for cancer therapies.
