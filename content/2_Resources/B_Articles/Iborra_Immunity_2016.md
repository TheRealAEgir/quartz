## Notes

> [!notes]
> %% begin Notes %%**Summary** : This article studied the impact of cross-presentation deficiency in DC (*via* DNGR1 and BATF3 KO mouse) on the T cell response, and found that it impair the TRM formation without impairing other memory Tcirc and effector T cell responses. *In vitro* priming of antigen-specific T cells with DC purified frmo WT/KO mouse showed that cross-presentation deficiency impaired Tbet, CXCR3 and FOXO1 expression, without impairing EOMES, T cells expansion and memory formation. *in vivo* Showed similar T cells responses efficiency, but there was less CD69+ TRM/antigen specific TRM in the skin, annd CD103 acquisition was slower. This effect was dependent on priming, as purification of primed T cells from WT/KO mouse and transfer in WT/KO mouse showed impairment only of T cells from KO mouse. This effect seemed due to early T cells egress from LN due to increase in S1PR1/KLF2 expression, and transiently blocking LN egress with FTY720 partially rescued the effects. *In vivo* priming with sorted DC population from WT/KO mouse, and transfer into mouse showed that CD11blowCD8alpha+ and CD103+ are better than CD11bhiCD8alpha- in a cross-presentation dependant manner at forming TRM, and this was due to type 3 signal including IL12, IL15 and CD24. [[TRM Priming]] [[TRM Differenciation]]
> <u>Importantly, the authors considered TRM as antigen-specific T cells in the skin based on the observation that most of these cells had a TRM phenotype.</u>
> 
>**Experiment** : VacV-OVA infection in WT or DNGR1 (CLEC9A) or BATF3 KO mouse, purification of CD11c+ DC and *in vitro* priming with OT-I or B8R-specific T cells
>**Results** : Intensity and persistence of antigen presentation was lower in both KO, leading to impaired Tbet/CXCR3 and FOXO1 expression with no impact on EOMES. There was no impact on T cells expansion nor memory TEM/TCM formation.
>
>**Experiment** : Re stimulation of dLN T cells with peptide from WT/DNGR1 KO mouse infected with skin VacV
>**Results** : There was similar INFg production, virus clearance and number of specific T cells in spleen/ovary in both primay and secondary response.
>**Other Results** : In the skin, there was less CD69+ TRM, less OT-I specific T cells and acquisition of CD103 was slower.
>**Experiment** : Infection in an ear, and re-challenge in both ears after 30 dpi with FTY720 treatment to remove Tcirc effect.
>**Results** : IFNg producing cells were only found in the previously infected ear in the WT mouse, and it had better virus titter.
>
>**Experiment** : To test if DNGR1/BATF3 defiency-induced TRM loss is important in priming and/or in skin differenciation, purification of primed OT-I cells from dLN WT/KO infected mouse and transfer into WT/KO mouse previoulsy infected
>**Results** : There was no impact in Tcirc number in all condition. Transfer of WT cells into WT/KO did not affect TRM number, but DNGR1 KO/BATF3 KO transfered into WT showed diminution in TRM, suggesting that priming impair TRM differenciation, and that KO DC in the skin does not have an effect.
>**Other Results** : Blocking of DNGR1 with neutralizing antibody during priming phase, but not sooner or latter, impacted TRM formation.
>
>**Other Results** : There was an increase in early effector OT-I T cell number in the skin in DNGR1 and BATF3 KO mouse, but these cells had KLRG1 and Tbet expression. There also was more proliferating T cells in the circulation in the KO mice. This was hypothesised as an early LN egress, and it was found that there was less S1PR1 and KLF2 mRNA expression in oT-I from KO mice. This was consolidated by a single FTY720 to transiently block LN egress, which partially rescued the effect observed in KO mice.
>
>**Experiment** : To determine which DC subset is important, infection of WT/KO mouse with VacV-OVA and sorting of CD11c cells from dLN into CD11bhiCD8alpha- and CD11blowCD8alpha+ (cDC) and CD103+ (migratory DC (mDC)).
>**Results** : CD11bhi cDC were less efficient at increasing IFNg in naive OT-I, and this was lost when using DC from DNGR1 KO mouse. Adding pre-processed OVA restored the effect.
>**Experiment** : OT-I from the previous experiment were transfered into previously infected mouse
>**Results** : Same Tcirc number no matter which DC subset is used. CD103+ and CD8aplha+ are more efficient than CD11bhi at forming TRM, and DNGR1 defiency affecter this effect. Adding of excess OVA peptide rescued it (bypass cross-presentation defiency).
>
>**Experiment** : Use of blocking antibody during *in vitro* priming with previously defined DC populations.
>**Results** : Less Tbet with CD11bhi, rescued by IL12/IL15/CD24 blockade. When transfering primed cell into a recipient mouse, IL12/IL15/CD24 blockade affected TRM but not memory Tcirc formation.
>
>**Other Results** : Validation of the results in another infection model with IA in the lung and NP/PA specific T cells, as well as in a i.n. VacV-NP model. This last one also protected against IA infection only in WT but not in DNGR1/BATF3 KO mouse.

> > [!Abstract]-
> >
> > Despite the crucial role of tissue-resident memory T (Trm) cells in protective immunity, their priming remains poorly understood. Here, we have shown differential priming requirements for Trm versus circulating memory CD8+ T cells. In vaccinia cutaneous-infected mice, DNGR-1-mediated crosspresentation was required for optimal Trm cell priming but not for their skin differentiation or for circulating memory T cell generation. DNGR-1+ dendritic cells (DCs) promoted T-bet transcription factor induction and retention of CD8+ T cells in the lymph nodes (LNs). Inhibition of LN egress enhanced Trm cell generation, whereas genetic or antibody blockade of DNGR-1 or specific signals provided during priming by DNGR-1+ DCs, such as interleukin-12 (IL-12), IL-15 or CD24, impaired Trm cell priming. DNGR-1 also regulated Trm cell generation during influenza infection. Moreover, protective immunity depended on optimal Trm cell induction by DNGR-1+ DCs. Our results reveal specific priming requirements for CD8+ Trm cells during viral infection and vaccination.
