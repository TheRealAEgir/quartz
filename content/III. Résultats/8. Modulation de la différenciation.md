We next sought whether these differentiation trajectories could be modulated _in vitro_. We observed several differences in TRM‑like subsets, including a high score of type I [[IFN]] signature in CXCR6-PD-1+ cells and high expression of the vitamin D receptor (VDR) in CXCR6+PD-1- cells ([[Figure 8 B.png]]), suggesting that the cell milieu can influence the differentiation. Therefore, cells were stimulated with multiple cytokines and molecules prior to generation of TRM-like cells ([[Figure 8 A.png]]). Interestingly, IFNβ stimulation significantly increased CXCR6-PD-1+ proportion while the use of an vitamin D analog (calcipotriol) increased the proportion of the CXCR6+PD-1- subtype ([[Figure 8 D.png]]). Together, our results showed that cellular state and cytokine environment have a major impact on the outcome of TRM-like generation, which itself is linked to the clinical outcome of patients under [[ICB]] treatment. Accordingly, we hypothesize that patients with response to [[ICB]] have differences in [[TCIRC]] compositions, namely [[TRM]] precursors, and the resulted [[TRM]] cells will have a major impact on the efficacy of [[ICB]] treatment.
